## Part I Ch 2: Arithmetic Expressions (source gegp102)
Concepts:
- C7M-2.1 Writing and comparing simple expressions. Model a real situation as an expression first; compare two expressions by looking at how their parts differ, not by computing both.
- C7M-2.2 Terms of an expression. Rewrite a - b as a + (-b) before identifying terms; a part with x or / and no + inside is one term.
- C7M-2.3 Commutative and associative property of addition (of terms). Terms can be swapped or regrouped in any order, also with negative terms.
- C7M-2.4 Evaluating expressions with brackets and mixed operations. Innermost bracket first; then each x or / term; then add all terms.
- C7M-2.5 Removing brackets (sign changes). a-(b+c)=a-b-c; a-(b-c)=a-b+c; a+(b-c)=a+b-c.
- C7M-2.6 Distributive property. a(b+c)=ab+ac, a(b-c)=ab-ac, both expanding and factoring.
- C7M-2.7 Real-world modelling and mental-math shortcuts. Rewrite a factor near a round number as round +/- small and distribute (97 x 25 = (100-3) x 25).
IN: model real situations (money, marbles, tickets) as expressions; different expressions can share a value; compare with =,<,> by structure; terms with "add the inverse"; commutative and associative property of terms; evaluate by computing x and / terms then adding; one level of brackets innermost-first; remove brackets after + (no sign change) and after - (flip every sign); distributive property expanding and in reverse; distributive property as a mental-math shortcut.
OUT: BODMAS/PEMDAS as a named acronym (the chapter teaches order via "terms"); variables/unknowns; solving equations; exponents; fractions or decimals inside expressions (whole numbers/integers only); nested brackets more than one level; new rules for multiplying negative numbers (Class 6 token model assumed); identities like (a+b) squared.

## Part I Ch 3: A Peek Beyond the Point (source gegp103)
Concepts:
- C7M-3.1 Reading & writing tenths and hundredths. 10 tenths = 1 unit; 10 hundredths = 1 tenth; convert mixed (3 4/10) and pure (34/10) forms.
- C7M-3.2 Decimal place value and notation. Each place past the point is 10 times smaller; 7.05 is "seven point zero five", read digit by digit.
- C7M-3.3 Trailing zeros and equivalent decimal forms. 0.2 = 0.20 = 0.200 but 0.2 != 0.02.
- C7M-3.4 Locating and comparing decimals. Zoom into segments on a number line; compare from the highest place, first difference decides.
- C7M-3.5 Unit conversions using decimals. 1 mm = 0.1 cm, 1 cm = 0.01 m, 1 g = 0.001 kg, 1 paisa = 0.01 rupee.
- C7M-3.6 Addition and subtraction of decimals. Align points, add/subtract column by column, carry/borrow across the point.
- C7M-3.7 Estimating sums and differences of decimals. whole1 + whole2 < sum < whole1 + whole2 + 2.
- C7M-3.8 Recognising non-decimal decimal-look-alike notation. Clock time (an hour is 60 min, not 10 tenths) and cricket overs (X.Y is X overs and Y balls out of 6).
- C7M-3.9 Closest decimals and decimal sequences. Closest = smallest difference from the target; in a sequence subtract neighbouring terms to find the change.
IN: tenths/hundredths as mixed and pure forms; add/subtract in tenths and hundredths with regrouping; place-value extension; reading decimals digit by digit; "234 tenths = 23.4"; mm/cm/m, g/kg, paise/rupee conversions; trailing zeros; number-line zooming; compare digit by digit; closest to a target; column addition/subtraction; extend a decimal sequence and find its constant change; estimate sums/differences by whole parts; clock time and cricket overs are not base-10 decimals.
OUT: percentages; recurring decimals; multiplication/division of decimals (Part II Ch 4); negative decimals; scientific notation; converting general fractions like 1/3 or 1/7 to decimals; rounding a decimal to a decimal place.

## Part I Ch 4: Expressions Using Letter-Numbers (source gegp104)
Concepts:
- C7M-4.1 Writing algebraic expressions from word descriptions. Name what varies with a letter, then write the operation described in words.
- C7M-4.2 Evaluating algebraic expressions by substitution. Substitute, then follow the order of operations.
- C7M-4.3 Omission of the multiplication symbol and term conventions. 4 times n is 4n; the number in front is the coefficient.
- C7M-4.4 Like terms and combining them. 5c + 3c + 10c = 18c; unlike terms cannot combine.
- C7M-4.5 Removing brackets and the distributive property. (40x+75y)-(6x+10y) = 34x+65y; 4(x+y)-y = 4x+3y.
- C7M-4.6 Testing whether two algebraic expressions are equal. Equal only if equal for EVERY value; two or more substitutions can disprove, never prove.
- C7M-4.7 Algebraic expressions for numeric and visual patterns. One expression for the nth term, found from the change per step and verified by substitution.
IN: letter-number for an unknown or varying quantity; expressions for ages, costs, perimeters, counts; substitution; repeated addition as multiplication; coefficient; swapping/grouping/brackets with a minus sign; like vs unlike terms; combining like terms; removing brackets including after a minus; simplest form; test equality by several substitutions; nth term of a numeric or visual pattern; position formula with remainder reasoning; a general expression proving a pattern always holds (calendar grid sum).
OUT: solving linear equations; multiplying two expressions (a+b)(c+d); powers like x squared; factorising as a generative skill; graphing; inequalities.

## Part I Ch 5: Parallel and Intersecting Lines (source gegp105)
Concepts:
- C7M-5.1 Linear pairs and vertically opposite angles. A linear pair sums to 180 degrees; vertically opposite angles are equal.
- C7M-5.2 Perpendicular lines. Intersecting lines forming four equal angles make 90 degrees each.
- C7M-5.3 Parallel lines: definition, identification and construction. Same plane, never meet; arrow marks.
- C7M-5.4 Transversals and the eight angles they form. Eight angles; four vertically opposite pairs, so at most four distinct measures.
- C7M-5.5 Corresponding angles and testing lines for parallelism. Equal corresponding angles prove parallel lines, and parallel lines give equal corresponding angles.
- C7M-5.6 Alternate angles. Equal on parallel lines (corresponding angle then vertically opposite).
- C7M-5.7 Co-interior angles and multi-step angle-chasing with parallel lines. Same-side interior angles sum to 180; chain linear pair, vertically opposite, corresponding, alternate and co-interior facts.
- C7M-5.8 Paper folding and parallel illusions. After n horizontal folds there are 2^n + 1 parallel lines (3, 5, 9, 17); slanted-looking lines can be parallel, judge by angle facts.
IN: four angles at an intersection; linear pair; vertically opposite with informal reasoning; perpendicular; parallel; construct parallels with ruler and set-square or folding; arrow and square-angle notation; transversal and its eight angles; corresponding, alternate and co-interior pairs; multi-step angle problems incl. more than one transversal; paper folding 2^n + 1; parallel illusions.
OUT: coordinate/slope tests; formal two-column proofs; skew lines/3D; compass constructions like perpendicular bisector; angle sum of a triangle or polygon.

## Part I Ch 6: Number Play (source gegp106)
Concepts:
- C7M-6.1 Sequences from heights (taller-in-front rule). A child's number counts taller children in front only; first and tallest say 0; the largest possible number in a group of n is n-1.
- C7M-6.2 Parity of sums, differences and products. even+even=even, odd+odd=even, even+odd=odd; a sum of k odd numbers is odd when k is odd; a product is odd only if every factor is odd.
- C7M-6.3 Parity of expressions and the nth even and odd number. 2n is the nth even, 2n-1 the nth odd; 3n+4 can be either.
- C7M-6.4 Row-and-column sum grids. Row sums total 45 and so do column sums; any row or column sum lies between 6 and 24.
- C7M-6.5 Magic squares. 3x3 with 1-9: magic sum 15, centre 5; centre m gives 3m for consecutive numbers; adding k adds 3k to the sum, multiplying by k multiplies the sum by k.
- C7M-6.6 Virahanka-Fibonacci numbers. Count for n = count for n-1 + count for n-2: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34; parities repeat odd, even, odd, odd, even, odd.
- C7M-6.7 Cryptarithms (digits in disguise). Same letter same digit, different letters different digits; units column first with carries; leading digit not 0.
IN: taller-in-front numbers and always/sometimes/never statements; parity by pairing pictures; possible-or-impossible sums (cards, ages, coins); parity of differences and products; parity of expressions; fill a 3x3 grid with given row/column sums; why magic sum 15 and centre 5 and why 1 and 9 cannot be in corners; build magic squares from consecutive numbers; Chautisa Yantra (4x4, sum 34); rhythms of short(1) and long(2) beats; extend Virahanka-Fibonacci both ways and its parity pattern; cryptarithms with carrying.
OUT: modular arithmetic notation/divisibility tests/remainders other than parity; counting arrangements with factorials; magic squares of order 5+ and general construction; golden ratio or closed form for Fibonacci; formal induction proofs.

## Part I Ch 7: A Tale of Three Intersecting Lines (source gegp107)
Concepts:
- C7M-7.1 Naming triangles and constructing equilateral triangles. Name vertices in any order; equal arcs from both ends of the base.
- C7M-7.2 Constructing a triangle from its three sides. Arcs of the other two lengths from the two ends; the third vertex is where they cross.
- C7M-7.3 The triangle inequality. Each side is less than the sum of the other two (check the longest only); direct path shorter than roundabout.
- C7M-7.4 Triangles from two sides and an angle, or two angles and a side. Two sides with the included angle < 180 always give a triangle; two angles with the included side only if they add to < 180.
- C7M-7.5 Angle sum property and exterior angles. Angles add to 180 (line through a vertex parallel to the opposite side); exterior angle = sum of the two opposite interior angles.
- C7M-7.6 Altitudes of a triangle. Perpendicular from a vertex to the opposite side (extended if needed); three altitudes; in a right triangle two are its sides.
- C7M-7.7 Types of triangles by sides and by angles. Equilateral/isosceles/scalene; acute/right/obtuse.
IN: naming; equilateral construction; three-sides construction; equilateral and isosceles from points on circles; triangle inequality with the touching, non-intersecting and intersecting circle cases; range of a third side; two sides and included angle construction; two angles and included side construction; angle sum with the parallel-line argument; exterior angle; altitudes of acute, obtuse, right triangles and construction with ruler and set-square; classification by sides and by angles.
OUT: congruence rules (SSS, SAS, ASA) and proofs (later chapter); larger angle opposite longer side and isosceles equal angles (later); Pythagoras; area of a triangle, medians, angle bisectors, perpendicular bisectors, triangle centres; the ambiguous two-sides-and-non-included-angle construction.

## Part I Ch 8: Working with Fractions (source gegp108)
Concepts:
- C7M-8.1 Multiplying a whole number and a fraction. Repeated addition; a/b x n = divide n by b then multiply by a; convert mixed fractions first.
- C7M-8.2 Multiplying two fractions with a unit square. Columns from one fraction, rows from the other; 1/b x 1/d = 1/(bd); rectangle area with fractional sides.
- C7M-8.3 Brahmagupta's multiplication rule and cancelling. a/b x c/d = ac/bd; a whole number is n/1; cancel common factors across the product.
- C7M-8.4 Size of a product and order of multiplication. Both > 1: product exceeds both; both between 0 and 1: product is less than both; one each: between them; order does not matter.
- C7M-8.5 Dividing fractions using reciprocals. Missing-factor idea; reciprocal of a/b is b/a; a/b divided by c/d = a/b x d/c.
- C7M-8.6 Size of a quotient compared with dividend and divisor. Divisor between 0 and 1 gives a bigger quotient; divisor > 1 a smaller one.
- C7M-8.7 Word problems and fraction-of-a-fraction chains. "A fraction of" means multiply; total = items x size of one; items = total / size of one.
IN: whole number x fraction as repeated addition and by divide-then-multiply; mixed to improper and back; unit-square multiplication; rectangle area with fractional sides; rows-and-columns for 1/b x 1/d; Brahmagupta's formula; cancelling before multiplying; word problems (mixed fractions, fraction of a fraction); product size comparisons; order of multiplication; division as missing factor and by reciprocal; quotient size comparisons; word problems (brick, fountain, cowrie shell, fractional relations square).
OUT: adding/subtracting fractions as its own topic; decimals, percentages, ratio and proportion in fraction problems; negative fractions; formal algebraic proofs of the formulas (the chapter uses pictures); division by zero.
