Tapestry of the Past
2 – Reshaping India’s Political Map

A new era in India’s journey began in the early 11th century.

Invasions from beyond the Hindu Kush mountains reshaped

India’s political map. No doubt, India had seen much warfare in

earlier periods, but the spate of invasions by people from outside

the Indian subcontinent during this period was unprecedented.

Many of these invaders were Central Asian — Turkic or Afghan.

They were drawn to India not only for her reputed riches and

for territorial ambitions, but also often to spread, by force of

violence if necessary, their own versions of their religion.

Let us now explore in this chapter the ever-changing landscape

of India from the 13th century onward.

Spellings, spellings …

Because of difficulties in transcribing the Persian script in

the Roman script, you will find some alternative spellings in

parentheses here and there. For instance, ‘Khalji’ or ‘Khilji’

are the same. Similarly, we use here the now standard spelling

of ‘Mughal’ spelling, but alternatives such as ‘Mughul’ or

‘Moghul’ are still sometimes used.
