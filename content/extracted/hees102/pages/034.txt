Akbar also instituted

the mansabdari system.

As Abul Fazl recorded

in his Ain-i-Akbari,

which describes

Akbar’s administration,

mansabdars (officers)

according to their mansab

(rank) were expected to

maintain a precise number

of elephants, horses,

camels as well as troops

for the state. This made

it possible to assemble

an army at a short notice

without having to maintain

a permanent centralised

army. Regular inspections

were carried out to ensure

compliance. Mansabdars

were generally paid

by being assigned land

(jagirs) and were therefore

also known as jagirdars.

Despite Akbar’s growing

tolerance for different

faiths, non-Muslims were

kept in a minority in

the higher echelons

of the administration;

for instance, the total

percentage of non-

Muslim officials in his

exceeded one-third of the total and was often much less. Even
