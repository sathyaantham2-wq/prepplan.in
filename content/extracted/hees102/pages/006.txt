LET’S EXPLORE

Looking at Fig. 2.6, why do you think Ala-ud-din Khilji called

himself ‘the second Alexander’?

At the turn of the 14th century, Ala-ud-din Khilji conducted

military campaigns over large areas of north and central India,

sacking and plundering many cities; at the same time, he also

repelled several invasions by Mongol forces, who were trying to

add India to the vast Mongol Empire (it covered most of Asia at

the time).

His slave-general Malik Kafur expanded the Sultanate’s reach

southward, conquering several kingdoms on the way; their

plundered wealth helped finance the Sultanate’s enormous

military apparatus. He also attacked a number of Hindu centres

such as Srirangam, Madurai, Chidambaram, and possibly

Rameswaram.
