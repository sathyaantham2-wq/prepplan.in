Exploring Society: India and Beyond | Grade 8 Part 1

From traders to rulers

The British conquest of India is one of history’s most remarkable

examples of how a trading company could transform into an

imperial power. Unlike classic conquests, the British takeover of

India was gradual, calculated, and often disguised as commercial

enterprise rather than military invasion.

The English East India Company was established as a trading

company and was granted a royal charter by Queen Elizabeth I,

which gave it special powers — to raise a private army, for

instance. Nevertheless, its agents initially kept up a pretence of

being mere traders, which allowed them, in the 17th century, to

establish footholds along India’s coast with minimal resistance:

Surat, Madras, Bombay and Calcutta among the first. Local rulers

did not mind these trading posts, as they generally welcomed

foreign trade (which, let us recall, was a longstanding practice in

India). These modest beginnings concealed the Company’s long-

term ambitions.

Before you read further, have a good look at the painting on

it, the objects, the symbols and the attitudes. Form groups of
