Tapestry of the Past
4 – The Colonial Era in India

Even earlier, there were several signs of severe discontent

among the sepoys, beginning with the so-called ‘Vellore Mutiny’

of 1806, which erupted when the British introduced new

uniform regulations that violated the religious practices of both

Hindu and Muslim sepoys. For example, they were forbidden

from wearing religious marks on their foreheads and were

required to shave their beards. The sepoys seized the Vellore

fort (in present-day Tamil Nadu) and killed many British officers

and troops; the British however crushed the revolt, killing or

executing hundreds of sepoys.

We should also keep in mind that most sepoys were from

agricultural families, which had been suffering great hardship

owing to the British policies on land revenue. After decades of

increasing frustration, in 1857 rumours spread across northern

and central India that rifle cartridges were greased with cow

and pig fat, offending the Hindu and Muslim sepoys’ religious

sensibilities. At Barrackpore (in present-day West Bengal), the
