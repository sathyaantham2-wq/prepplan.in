Wells have been

constructed in many

sizes and shapes from

bricks, stones, or

terracotta ring. In

classical times, a

tradition of stepwells

(vāvs or bāorīs)

developed in Gujarat

and Rajasthan, many of them engineering marvels as well

as spectacular works of art. The stepwell at Rani ki Vav

(Fig. 3.16) has multiple pillared levels and hundreds of

magnificent statues. Besides harvesting and storing rainwater,

stepwells offered a social

space where people

could sit together, enjoy

cooler temperatures and

exchange all the news.

Temples often had water

reservoirs or puṣ hkariṇīs

attached to them, which

were used for water

harvesting as well as

water-based rituals. Many

of them were also works

of art, with numerous

stairways built into them

(Fig. 3.17).

Many of these traditional water structures suffer today from

depleted water sources, maintenance issues, pollution, waste

dumping, etc. Environmentalists, locals, and NGOs sometimes

join hands to preserve them, but much remains to be done.
