Structural Stupa Architecture

Fig. 3.8. A view of the main stupa at Sanchi, with

toraṇas and an enclosure of railings.

From the 4
th 
or 3
rd 
century BCE onward,

with the rise of Buddhism several new

architectural forms emerged in north

India, slowly spreading to other regions.

Thousands of stupas (dome-shaped

structures housing relics of the Buddha

or revered monks) were constructed with

bricks or stone. The larger ones became

complex ritual centres with railings,

gateways, and elaborate panels often

narrating episodes in the Buddha’s life.

Fig. 3.9. A panel at Bharhut depicting

people worshipping the stupa there.

At this early stage, the Buddha was represented symbolically—

through objects such as footprints, the wheel of dharma, or the

stupa itself—rather than in human form.

stupa at Sanchi (Figs. 3.8 and 3.10) in Madhya Pradesh represents

the earliest phase of monumental religious stone architecture in
