The Jama Masjid in Delhi (Figs. 3.31 and 3.32) is India’s largest

mosque. Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan built it in the mid-17
th

century, after he moved his capital from Agra to Shahjahanabad

(now Old Delhi). He had a vision

for a grand mosque where the

city’s Muslims could gather for

prayer. About 5,000 workers

were pressed into service for

its construction.

The mosque is made from red

sandstone with white marble

decorations, and has three large

entrances. Two 40-metre tall

minarets stand on either side.

The main prayer hall has three

large domes and the red-stone

courtyard can accommodate

up to 25,000 people. The walls

and arches are decorated with

calligraphy (decorative writing)

of verses from the Qur’an.
