Belief Systems

During this time, religious differences between the invaders and

the local population were striking. The difference between a

monotheistic Islam with one prophet, and a religion with several

schools of thought and no single holy book, was quite stark at

the time. The desire of many of the invaders to annihilate the

‘infidels’ — guided by their own version of Islam — led to the

plunder and destruction of some of the oldest, and most sacred,

sites and temples.

Naturally, these differences led to various complexities; for a

time, one may have felt that there could be no meeting ground,

or that what had happened in other parts of the world, where

rulers guided by their own militant versions of their faith had

suppressed the indigenous faiths, might recur here. And yet,

more than a millennium after the first encounters with such

invaders, the belief systems native to India continue, and Islam

also continues to be practised in India along with other religions.

What was it about the culture of India that enabled society to

weather that stormy time, and for multiple diverse groups of

people to continue to coexist in the subcontinent?
