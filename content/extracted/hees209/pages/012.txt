Atelier:

An artist’s

studio

Mughal miniature painting flourished during this period as a

genre distinct from Rajput art, shaped by Persian influences.

These works depicted secular themes — landscapes, court

scenes, and portraits. Known as court paintings, created due to

Mughal royal patronage, they reached their height in Akbar’s

atelier, where collaborations among artists were common — one

artist would draw the lines, another fill the colours, and a third

add finishing touches. Miniatures also appeared in manuscripts,

including Persian translations of the Mahā bhā rata.
