Pashmina shawls are renowned for their softness and warmth.

They are woven from cashmere, a type of soft wool from the

goats of Ladakh and Kashmir. During the Mughal era, they

gained the patronage of the emperors and became a symbol of

nobility. They were exported to other countries in Asia and were

in demand among fashionable Europeans too. The embroidery

shows distinct Persian influence, featuring floral motifs.

Fig. 8.28. The Persian buteh or būtā (paisley) motif in a pashmina shawl. The

būtā is like a teardrop with a curved end.
