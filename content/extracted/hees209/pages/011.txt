The other stream, Carnatic music (we referred to this when

we met Purandaradāsa in the previous section) continued to

develop independently, largely unaffected by Persian and Arabic

influences.

This period also saw the adaptation of instruments that continue

to be used today. While exact origins remain debated by scholars,

modern forms of instruments like the sitar and tabla evolved

during these centuries through experimentation by instrument

makers and musicians from various backgrounds, even while

the musical repertoires of these instruments came from the

traditions of much older Indian instruments, e.g., the ancient

pakhāwaj in the case of the tabla.
