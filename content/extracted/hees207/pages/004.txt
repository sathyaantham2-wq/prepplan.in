How do Cities Become Centres

of Economic Activities?

Throughout history, we have seen places grow to become prominent

urban centres through the advantages of location near rivers that

afforded ease of transportation, at the crossroads of trade routes

that facilitated the development of markets, as well as ease of

access to agricultural produce and raw materials that supported

the development of artisan communities and manufacturing.

They became centres of economic activities as well as political and

administrative power.

According to the United Nations’ estimates, by 2050, 68 per cent of the

world’s population will be living in cities, compared to the 55 per cent

now. A similar trend is expected in India, where the urban population

has grown from 17 per cent in 1951 to around 40 per cent in 2025.
