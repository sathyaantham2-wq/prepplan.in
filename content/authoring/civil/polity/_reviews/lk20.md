## HIGH
- **CS-POL-ch20-077** (stmt12, key "both correct and Statement-II explains Statement-I").
  I: PM is leader of the Lower House. II: PM can recommend dissolution of the Lok Sabha at any time.
  The book (p.310) says "In this capacity [leader of the Lower House] he/she enjoys the following powers", so the leadership is the
  reason for the power, not the other way round. "II explains I" has the direction reversed; "both correct, II does not explain I"
  is at least as defensible. Two defensible answers. Fix: swap the statements (I = dissolution power, II = leader of the Lower House),
  or key it "does not explain" and rewrite the explanation/w.
- **CS-POL-ch20-097** (stmt12, key "II explains I").
  I: observers like to call the British system "Prime Ministerial government". II: Crossman said the post-war epoch has been the final
  transformation of cabinet government into Prime Ministerial government. Crossman's line is one observer's remark that illustrates the
  label, not the reason observers use it; the author's own explanation says "as support", and "support" is not "explain".
  "Does not explain" is equally defensible. Fix: make II a genuine cause stated by the page (e.g. Berkely's "super-ministerial powers"
  as the basic defect) or key as "does not explain".
