## HIGH

**CS-POL-ncp1-2-11** (which-statements, key "2 only")
Problem: Statement 1 "Fundamental rights are protected in a democracy by the executive, which is the organ that implements the law" is declared false only because the chapter assigns protection to the judiciary (silence/emphasis). In real law the executive is bound by Part III (Art. 12/13) and the police and administration protect rights daily, so a candidate can defend statement 1 as correct, which makes "1 and 2 only" defensible. This violates the Falsifier rule.
Fix: Make statement 1 false in real law, for example "Fundamental rights are protected in a democracy only by the legislature, and the judiciary has no role in protecting them", or "Only the executive can enforce fundamental rights against the State".

**CS-POL-ncp1-2-17** (how-many, key "Only two")
Problem: Statement 3 "Equality, freedom and universal adult franchise differ from one democracy to another" is marked false on the basis of p.196 ("remain the same in all democracies"). But the same chapter (pp.192-193, and statement 2 of this item) says the principles evolved, that not all democracies put them into practice, and gives Switzerland (women's vote 1971) and the UK/USA dates. A candidate can read statement 3 as true in practice, making the count three, so two answers are defensible.
Fix: Reword statement 3 to something false in real terms, for example "Universal adult franchise was available to all adults from the beginning in every democracy", or drop it and add a plainly false statement.

## MEDIUM

**CS-POL-ncp1-1-03** Real-law mislead: "an authority penalises owners who flout it" is keyed as the judicial function, but penalising by a transport authority is executive enforcement in real law; only the chapter's loose "ensuring rules are followed" wording makes it judicial. Fix: make the third act "a court hears the case and penalises the owners".

**CS-POL-ncp1-5-13** Loose page. Statements 2 and 3 (42nd Amendment adds "secular", Arts 25-28, positive secularism) are on Laxmikanth pdf p.86, not p.87; p.87 has no secularism text. pg and explanation cite 87. Fix: pg [86], and "p.86" in e and w.

**CS-POL-ncp1-7-19** Assertion-reason "II explains I": "SC is yet to define the basic structure" and "the elements emerged from judgements" are two sentences of the same passage; whether II explains I is a judgement call (a reviewer could say both true, II does not explain). Fix: re-angle so one statement is false, or use two independent true facts and key "does not explain".

**CS-POL-ncp1-7-04** Weak distractors: options 1-3 (political democracy alone; states may secede; executive decides rights) are absurd and eliminated without the fact. Fix: use plausible near-misses (for example other Preamble terms from p.87: popular sovereignty, social democracy as a way of life).

**CS-POL-ncp1-2-05** Weak distractors: the "1971 for India / 1950 for Swiss women" option is absurd, and three of four options repeat the same stem with swapped years. Fix: vary the year pairs (1971 vs 1928 vs 1945 vs 1920).

**CS-POL-ncp1-7-03** Key "elections held at regular and fixed intervals" is obviously not a problem, so the item is solved by common sense. Fix: make the odd one a subtler item (for example "universal adult franchise" or "an independent judiciary") that the chapter does not list as a problem.

Fix: replace with items on the chapter's own material (Table 9.2, accountability, role of citizens) or retire the duplicated ones.
