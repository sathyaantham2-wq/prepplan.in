**M4 - dangling reference in CS-POL-ch07-117 (7.1, item 16, st 1):** "The Attorney General of India is not covered by this list,
which is confined to constitutional heads such as President and Governor." The stem shows no list, so the statement can only be
judged by the reader guessing what "this list" is; it is false only because of a phrase with no antecedent. Rewrite as "Only
constitutional heads such as the President and the Governor are among the offices for which citizens are eligible and aliens
are not."

**M5 - CS-POL-ch07-214 (7.2, item 13), statement 3 is false only by a qualifier.** "Where the Constitution gives rights to
citizens, a state in India may still prefer its own residents in those matters." The chapter allows preference in matters
outside the Constitution's rights (p.111). In real law domicile preference in education is partly permitted, so the falsifier
is a close call that a well-read candidate could read as true. The key (2 only) still stands, but replace the statement with
one that is clearly false (for example "A state may offer public-employment preference to its residents without any law of Parliament").

**M6 - CS-POL-ch07-411 (7.4, item 10), statement 1 is loosely worded and the key depends on it being true.** "persons who
migrated to Pakistan and returned after 26 January 1950" can be read as "returned after" only. Article 7 covered returnees
under a resettlement permit whenever the permit issued, so a reader could call it false in law, leaving no correct option. The
chapter (fn 5) speaks of persons who *migrated* after 26 January 1950. Reword to "migrated to Pakistan after 26 January 1950 and
returned".
