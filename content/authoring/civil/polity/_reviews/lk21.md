## The author's five least-sure facts
1. **Binding advice / proviso (42nd v 44th): consistent, no defect.** p.316 credits both amendments with "binding"; footnote 1 (p.323) splits them. Every item stays inside the
   footnote: 705 (proviso = 44th, 1978; real law also 44th), 708 (st.1 42nd, st.2 44th proviso, st.3 act after reconsideration), 717 st.4 ("42nd made the advice binding", true in the footnote and in real law),
   718 (II = 44th proviso, binds after reconsideration, explanation is sound). No item asserts that the 44th alone made advice binding, and none asserts that the 42nd added the proviso as true.
2. **Devi Lal 1990: fine.** 738 st.II "the Constitution expressly provides for the office of Deputy PM" is false on p.317 ("of which there is no mention in the Constitution") and in real law
   (Art 75 speaks only of the PM and other Ministers). 732 st.2 is framed as the ground of the challenge (true as history). 736 st.1 is bare ("The Constitution provides only for the Prime Minister and ministers"),
   true in the real law and the page does not contradict it; key "Only two" stands. See L4 on the "wrong description" paraphrase in 736 st.3.
3. **752 st.3 "President is bound to dissolve whenever a council that lost confidence advises it": false in real law as well as on p.318.** Art 74 binds the President to the council's advice,
   but the settled convention (and the 1979 and 1998 precedents) is that the President may refuse or explore alternatives when the council has lost its majority. The word "whenever" also makes it categorical against "may not oblige". Key "1 only" is safe.
4. **754 st.3 "the President can be sued in the Supreme Court for an order that violates the law": false in real law (Art 361(1): the President is not answerable to any court for the exercise of official powers).**
   But the explanation says "the chapter says nothing of the sort", which is the recurring defect in the explanation even though the falsifier is true. See M1.
5. **722 "NOT in the oath": correct in real law.** Third Schedule oath of a Union minister: true faith and allegiance to the Constitution; uphold the sovereignty and integrity of India; faithfully and conscientiously discharge duties;
   do right to all manner of people in accordance with the Constitution and the law without fear or favour, affection or ill-will. Defending Fundamental Rights against the legislature is in no limb. The four-limb list on p.317 matches the Schedule. Key correct.

## HIGH

Ambedkar resigned from the Union cabinet on 27 September 1951 over the stalled Hindu Code Bill. "1953" is an error in Laxmikanth's own text (p.318) which the pack copied into a statement it marks TRUE.
- 749 (key "1 and 3 only"): if st.1 is false the true statement set is {3}, and "3 only" is not among the options, so a student who knows the history has no correct option.
- 755 (key "Only one"): st.1 is the only statement counted correct. With the year false, the answer would be "None", which is option B. Two defensible answers, one of them contradicting the key.
- Same year also sits in 742 option B "Hindu Code Bill of 1953" and its w-note (harmless as a distractor but repeats the error).
Fix: drop the year (and "1953" in 742 B and its w-note): "Dr B.R. Ambedkar resigned over the Hindu Code Bill." The book fact the pack wants (reason for resignation) is unchanged.
