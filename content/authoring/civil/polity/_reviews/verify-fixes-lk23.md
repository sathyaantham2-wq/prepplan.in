## HIGH

**H1 - 309 (23.3), statement 3 is true in real law, so the keyed combination is not the only defensible reading.**
Statement 3 reads "Parliament must meet at least once a year" and is marked incorrect because the page says "at least twice a year"
(p.343). "At least once" is a weaker but TRUE claim (twice implies once). With statements 1, 2, 4 true (p.343: President summons;
six-month gap; "usually three sessions"), a careful student finds all four true and no option "1, 2, 3 and 4"; the key (1, 2 and 4)
rests on a wording trick, not on knowledge. Fix: make the falsifier actually false, e.g. "Parliament must meet at least three times
a year" or "The gap between two sessions can be as long as one year". Also still a near-copy of UPSC-POL-2020-392 (st 1 same
wording), which the earlier review (M12/M13) asked to vary.

**H2 - 919 (23.9), Statement-I "The President has an absolute veto over an ordinary bill passed by both Houses" keyed incorrect.**
The pack's own 912 statement 4 is keyed TRUE ("If the President withholds assent, the bill ends", p.358), and Laxmikanth's veto
discussion (absolute veto = withholding assent) makes Statement-I defensible as correct. The key rests on the single line
"the President enjoys only a suspensive veto" (p.358). A well-read student can validly answer C (both correct, II does not explain I).
That is two defensible answers. Fix: re-word Statement-I so it is plainly false by the chapter, e.g. "The President can refuse assent
to an ordinary bill that both Houses have passed a second time after his return" (false, p.358), keeping Statement-II.
(Not on the author's list; not raised in lk23-b.)

## MEDIUM

**M1 - Match items: 11 of 12 keys are the identical text "A-1, B-2, C-3" (only 23.2 differs), and the key is the identity mapping.**
Every distractor shares at least one pair with the key, so the key is the "consensus" option. A student who knows one pair can
triangulate, and a blind guess of A-1, B-2, C-3 scores 92% over the pack. Not caught by check-civil-pack.py (it checks only
which/howmany). Fix: permute List-II in at least 6 of the 12 items so the key varies (e.g. 3-1-2, 2-3-1, 1-3-2).

**M2 - 218 (23.2) "Statement-II explains Statement-I?" is a judgement call.** Statement-II is the definition of the territorial
system; Statement-I is that the Constitution adopted FPTP. Keyed "both correct, II does not explain I". A reasonable student can say
II explains what FPTP is. Make II a clearly non-explanatory true fact (e.g. the 61st Amendment lowered the voting age) or an
explicit explanation. The other 'explains' calls (319, 418, 518, 618, 718, 918, 1018, 1118, 1218) are sound.

**M3 - 415 (23.4) st 3 turns on 2010 vs 2018 while the stem frames every statement "as raised in 2018".** Fair as a date test
(p.335 "Earlier in 2010"), but the frame invites reading the present daily allowance as part of the 2018 package. Also see M6.
Consider "The daily allowance was increased from Rs 1,000 to Rs 2,000 before 2018" as the true statement and an invented figure
as the falsifier, or drop the 2018 frame for st 3.
