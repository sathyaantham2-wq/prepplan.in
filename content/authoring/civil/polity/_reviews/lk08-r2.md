### MEDIUM
1. **098 (relation key is contestable).** Key: "Both correct, Statement-II does not explain Statement-I". A candidate can argue II ("the external dimension is dealt with by Article 21") is the reason Article 19 omits it, because the page puts it as "Article 19 protects only the first dimension. The second dimension is dealt by Article 21". The author applied the reviewer's earlier suggested fix, so it is consistent with 018/039/058, and I read II as a placement fact rather than a cause. I do not call it two defensible answers, but it is the weakest relation item in the pack. Optional hardening: give II a sentence that is clearly a different fact, or make I and II about a neutral pair.
2. **No "1, 2, 3 and 4" key among 65 four-statement "which" items.**
   - Key shapes for the 65 four-statement items: size 3 = 32, size 2 = 28, size 1 = 5, size 4 = 0. For the 25 three-statement items: size 3 = 4, size 2 = 18, size 1 = 3.
   - "1, 2, 3 and 4" appears as an option (010, 046, 066, 068, 133 ...) but is never the key in a four-statement item, so a student who learns to reject the all-true option is rewarded.
   - Real papers sometimes key all-true. Fix by turning one or two four-statement items into all-true keys. Candidates: 026 (replace st.1 with a true statement), 066, 070 (make st.4 true, "covers economic compulsion").
3. **Two-statement keys are 46 of 90 "which" items (51%).** The 28 four-statement and 18 three-statement items with a two-item key are the largest class. Lone-size keys are 8/90 (9%: 4 are "1 only", 3 "2 only", 1 "3 only"), which is acceptable. Mix the key sizes a little more as in MEDIUM 2. Not a correctness issue.

### LOW
1. **127 pg.** pg [148,149] omits p.144, which carries the "right against bundh" fact used to falsify st.4. Add 144. Also 130's w-note cites p.148 where the facts are on p.149.
2. **050 st.3 is time-sensitive.** The 2019 property/asset tests were dropped for EWS after the Ajay Bhushan Pandey committee (Dec 2021 / Jan 2022 government acceptance). The stem anchors to "according to Laxmikanth", which is why this is not a HIGH. st.1 (Rs 8 lakh), st.2 and st.4 remain true or false today as keyed. Optionally add "as laid down in 2019".
3. **116 st.4** (Britain "permanent feature of peace-time law") is false per the page ("only during first and second world war time"). In real history Britain also used internment in Northern Ireland (1971) and detention under the 2001 anti-terror law, so the book's "only in wartime" is itself loose. The statement as worded is still false in real law, so the key holds. Consider "was used in Britain only in war time" if the author wants to avoid the nuance.
4. **`tr` (trap tags) is empty on the 10 match items.** The checker allows it.
5. **Repetition** (ideas at 3 or more within a concept):
   - Article 32 "may not refuse / is itself a Fundamental Right" vs Article 226 discretionary: 165, 176 st.4, 178, 179 (4 items, one idea).
   - Article 21A "elementary only, not higher education": 133 st.2, 136 st.1, 139 (3).
   - Article 24 "harmless work not prohibited": 063, 072 st.1, 079 (3).
   - Article 361 immunity details: 025, 032, 035 (3, different clauses).
   - Preventive detention three months / advisory board: 103, 111, 112 (3, different sub-facts).
   - Awards/titles: 062, 068, 069, 078 (4, different sub-facts).
   - Only the first group is a near-identical concept. Swap one of 165/178/179 for another Article 32 fact.
6. **089 st.1** "can be exercised only on public land" is book wording; the stem says "as described in the chapter" so it is anchored.
