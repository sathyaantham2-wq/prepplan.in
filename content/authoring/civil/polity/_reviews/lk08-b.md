## HIGH
**H1 - 114 (CS-POL-ch08-114), statement 3 is false in real law yet is a "correct" statement in the key.**
"No democratic country in the world has made preventive detention an integral part of its Constitution as India has done"
(book p.153, repeated p.167). Preventive detention is written into the constitutions of Pakistan (Art 10), Bangladesh (Art 33),
Malaysia (Art 151) and Singapore (Art 151), all of which a well-read aspirant can name. The key is "1, 2 and 3"; an informed
student picks "1 and 2 only" and is defensible. This is the recurring defect (the book's claim copied as fact). Fix: replace
st 3 with a safe sentence, for example "Article 22 has two parts, the first for ordinary law and the second for preventive
detention (p.152)", or "Preventive detention existed in British India, for example under the Bengal State Prisoners
Regulation of 1818 (p.153)" (but that fact is also in 116 st 3).

**H2 - 155 (CS-POL-ch08-155), statement 1 is internally inconsistent, so "Only two" versus "Only one" is arguable.**
"They are of three types, according to whether they seek recognition, aid, both or neither." That lists four categories
(recognition, aid, both, neither) for "three types". The book's three types (p.157) are: recognition and aid; recognition only;
neither. "Aid only" is not a type. A careful reader marks st 1 false and then the key is "Only one"; a lenient reader marks it
true and the key is "Only two". Fix: "They are of three types: those that seek both recognition and aid, those that seek
recognition only, and those that seek neither."

## MEDIUM
**M1 - 130 (CS-POL-ch08-130), statement 3 mixes a true right with a false rider under a "rights declared to be part of
Article 21" stem.** "Right to travel abroad, which falls under Article 19(1)(d)." The right to travel abroad is on the list (p.149,
Satwant Singh p.150) and p.146 says Art 19 covers only the internal dimension, so the key (1, 2 and 4) is right. But under the
stem's frame ("rights declared ... part of Article 21") a student can read st 3 as true and choose "1, 2, 3 and 4". Fix: drop the
rider, make st 3 a plainly wrong right (for example "Right to strike") and keep the travel-abroad point for a separate item.

**M2 - 158 and 198: "Statement-II explains Statement-I" is arguable.**
158: St I says the right to propagate does not include converting another person; St II says forcible conversions impinge on
freedom of conscience. II covers only forcible conversion, I covers all conversion, so "correct but does not explain" is also
defensible. 198: St II (objective of Art 33 is discipline in the forces) is the purpose of the power, not the reason why the law
cannot be challenged (that is the text of Art 33), so again both relation options are arguable. 178 and 138 are sound (the
book itself gives II as the reason). Fix 158/198: use a pair where II is plainly the cause, or make the relation clearly
"correct, but not an explanation" (a fact that is true and unrelated).

**M3 - 196 statement 4: false only by inference from the book.** The book says laws included "after 24 April 1973" are valid
only if they do not damage the basic structure (p.165); it does not say pre-1973 laws are closed. The author's explanation
("the cut-off date applies to the validity test") is a restatement, not a page fact. Real law (Waman Rao) does close them, so
the key is right, but the page does not state it. Fix: reword st 4 as "all laws in the Ninth Schedule, whatever the date of
inclusion, are valid only if they do not damage the basic structure", which p.165-166 contradict directly.

**M4 - longest-key tell in direct items: 11 of 25 (44%, chance 25%).** The key is the single longest option in 103, 105, 124,
125, 141, 142, 144, 161, 165, 181, 183. Worst: 141 (key is a full clause, distractors are short), 161, 165, 124. Lengthen
the distractors to the same shape.

**M5 - "all correct" three-statement items always key the unique all-three option.** 114, 127, 152, 174, 193 (key "1, 2 and 3",
the other options are pairs). A student who sees three plausible statements picks the odd-sized option and is right five times
