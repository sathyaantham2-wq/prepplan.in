## HIGH
**H1 - CS-POL-ch18-513 (LK-18.5): statement 1 "Under Article 200 the Governor has three alternatives" is marked false, but it
is defensible as true.** Key "2 and 4 only"; the option "1, 2 and 4 only" is then equally right.
- Laxmikanth counts four by splitting "withhold assent" from "return the bill" (p.289). But Article 200's text has three
  declarations (assents, withholds assent, reserves for the President) plus a proviso allowing return of a non-Money Bill.
  The Supreme Court, in the 2025 Tamil Nadu Governor judgment and the Presidential Reference opinion (Nov 2025, as I recall;
  please confirm), speaks of the Governor's three options. The stem does not say "according to the chapter".
- Fix: change statement 1 to something with no count (for example "The Governor may reserve the bill for the President"
  and make the key follow), or add "According to Laxmikanth" and keep four; the first is safer because 515 st 2 and 519 also
  rest on the same area (M2).

**H2 - CS-POL-ch18-416 (LK-18.4): statement 1 "International treaties concluded on his behalf need no approval of Parliament"
is marked false, but it is true in constitutional practice.** Key "None"; the real-law answer is "Only one".
- p.286 says treaties "are subject to the approval of the Parliament". The Constitution contains no such requirement:
  treaty-making is an executive power (Art 73 and 53); Parliament's role starts only with legislation to implement a treaty
  (Art 253). Standard constitutional texts and practice say exactly this, and UPSC aspirants are taught it. The stem has no
  "according to the chapter" qualifier.
- Fix: drop the treaty statement (use a different falsifier, for example "he appoints the Prime Minister's secretary"
  style statements from p.283) or tag the stem "according to the chapter".

**M1 - CS-POL-ch18-411 (LK-18.4), st 3 "declare war or conclude peace without the approval of Parliament" - the same book-vs-law
gap as H2.** Nothing in the Constitution requires Parliament's approval (Art 53(2) leaves the use of the forces to law, and no

**M2 - pocket-veto items state a contested position as plain fact (CS-POL-ch18-515 st 2, 519 st 1, also 505 and 513 st 4).**
The Constitution prescribes no time limit (that part is certain), and the book says the President "can exercise the pocket
veto". The Supreme Court's 2025 rulings on Governor and President delay (April 2025 judgment, then the Nov 2025 Presidential
Reference opinion) held that courts cannot fix timelines but may order a decision on inordinate unexplained delay, and
rejected "deemed assent". Whether an indefinite pocket veto is available is therefore not settled as plainly as the item
says (key of 515 "Only two" and 519 "II explains I" depend on it). Reword to "According to the chapter", or add a
2025-aware item. Also 515 st 3 "The President can himself return a reserved bill" is a wording trap (the book says he
directs the Governor to return it); a reading trick rather than a fact test.

**M3 - CS-POL-ch18-701 / 710 / 720: respite, reprieve and commutation overlap.** 701 describes "a lesser sentence in place of the
one originally awarded because of pregnancy" and offers commutation ("substitution ... for a lighter form") as an option.
Only the "special fact" separates them, and in ordinary legal usage pregnancy is the textbook case for a reprieve (stay of
execution). The stem says "in the terminology of the chapter", which rescues 701; 710 (st 1 and st 2) has no such
qualifier, though the key (2, 3 and 4 only) survives either reading. Add the qualifier to 710.

**M4 - CS-POL-ch18-712, st 3 "Maru Ram also held no right to an oral hearing" is false only by the book's attribution.**

student cannot verify the negative from the case itself. Same for 715 st 3 (1980 date is fine). Add the qualifier or use
