## HIGH

**CS-POL-ch34-091 (LK-34.5, "which", key `1 and 3 only`) - statement 2 is true in real law; stem not hedged.**
St2: "An aggrieved person may approach the Supreme Court directly against a tribunal's decision without first going to the high
court." The key marks it false (e says "the high court must be approached first"). That is the book's generalisation of Chandra Kumar
(p.514). In real law many tribunals have a statutory appeal straight to the Supreme Court (NGT, TDSAT, NCLAT, Armed Forces Tribunal,
SAT, and others), so a well-read aspirant can defend st2 as true and `1, 2 and 3 only` as correct. This is the recurring defect (false only
because the book generalises). Fix: open the stem "According to the chapter, consider..." and phrase st2 as "Under the chapter, a person
aggrieved by a tribunal's decision can go to the Supreme Court without first approaching the high court" (still false by p.514), or drop
st2 for a clean falsifier (for example "tribunals are outside the writ jurisdiction of the high courts"). Same hedge for **090** st3 (see M5).

**CS-POL-ch34-095 (LK-34.5, "howmany", key `Only three statements`) - st3 names a repealed statute as currently governing.**
St3: "governed in part by the Criminal Procedure Code, 1973 and the Civil Procedure Code, 1908." The Criminal Procedure Code, 1973 was
repealed by the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (in force 1 July 2024) and the IPC by the BNS; only the CPC 1908 survives. Today (2026)
a current-law reader can call st3 false (or half false) and answer `Only two statements`. The book (8e) pre-dates the change, so the pack
needs its own safeguard. Fix: stem "According to the chapter, ..." with st3 unchanged, or replace st3 by "Letters Patent" only and
use another true source (the chapter's list (a)-(g) has constitutional provisions, Letters Patent, Acts of Parliament, Acts of State Legislature,
CPC 1908). Also add a "(since replaced by the BNSS 2023)" clause to the explanation.
