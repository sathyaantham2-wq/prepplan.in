## HIGH

**607 - unattributed "records above Rs 2,000" (two defensible answers).** Stem has no "according to the chapter". Statement 3
("Parties must keep records of persons making donations above Rs 2,000") is true only because p.832 says so. In real law the
cash limit is Rs 2,000 but the record and reporting threshold for party donations stays Rs 20,000 (RP Act s.29C, Income Tax
Act s.13A). A candidate who knows the law answers "1 and 4 only" (option 2), which is on the page. Fix: open the stem with
"According to the chapter" and reword statement 3 "the chapter says parties must keep records ...".

**608 - states "now" a position the Supreme Court struck down.** Statement 2 "A company can now donate any amount to any
political party" is key-true, statement 3 "The company must still report ..." is key-false. In *ADR v Union of India*
(15 Feb 2024) the Court held the 2017 removal of the 7.5 per cent cap (Companies Act s.182) and of the profit-and-loss
disclosure unconstitutional, so in current law statement 2 is false and statement 3 is true: the key inverts. The stem says
"in the 2017 budget" but the word "now" asserts a present state. Fix: "According to the chapter", past tense ("the 2017
budget removed ...; the chapter says the duty to report was lifted"), as ncert_polity5 ncp5-6-12 already does.

**611 - "Parties can now receive funds from foreign companies" stated as fact.** Key-true statement 2 (2,3,4 correct) has no
attribution. Real law: FCRA 2010 s.3 still bars political parties from foreign contribution; the 2016 and 2018 Finance Act
changes (retrospective redefinition of "foreign source") let parties take money from Indian-registered companies with foreign
shareholding, not from foreign companies as such. The book's sentence is a loose paraphrase. Fix: "According to the chapter"
in the stem, or reword statement 2 to "the chapter says parties can now receive funds from foreign companies".

**618 - Statement-I repeats the same unattributed claim.** "The 2018 budget allowed political parties to receive funds from
foreign companies" is the key-correct Statement-I. Same fix as 611. The "II explains I" relation rests on the book's word
"Accordingly" (p.833); defensible but see L5.

## MEDIUM

**M1 - Death of a candidate (209, 204, LK-83.2 rule sentence).** The chapter (p.827) says the poll is not countermanded and a
recognised party gets seven days. UPSC 1999 Q53 statement IV accepted that wording for the 1996 Act, so the key is
defensible, and 209 is attributed ("According to the chapter"). But current practice under s.52 is that the poll IS
countermanded when a recognised party's candidate dies (Jayanagar 2018, Betul 2024); statement 2 would be false for a
reader using current law and "1 and 4 only" is an option. The false statements (30 days, whichever is more) are false in the
chapter and in real law. Fix: keep "as the 1996 reform provided / the chapter says" in 209 and 204 and add a clause to the
83.2 rule sentence that current practice countermands for recognised-party candidates.

**M2 - Metadata LK-83.6 (description, idea, rule) carries no status note.** Rule sentences on the 2017 corporate cap, the
2017 cash limit and the 2018 bond scheme are accurate history, and the bond sentence is hedged ("The chapter describes"),
but the idea "party funding rules were loosened" and the corporate-cap sentence are not true in current law after Feb 2024
(bond scheme and cap removal struck down). The loader creates these rows. Fix: add "(struck down by the Supreme Court in
February 2024; the chapter was written earlier)" to the rule, and align scope_out wording.
