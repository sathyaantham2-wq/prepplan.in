## HIGH

**CS-POL-ch24-216 (LK-24.2, how many; key "Only one statement").** Statement 4 reads "It was first established in the post-independence
era, in 1950." The key treats it as false because the book says the PAC was set up in 1921. But this is the recurring defect: it is false
only because of the book's wording. In real history the PAC was constituted afresh as a parliamentary committee in 1950, after the
Constitution came into force, so a well-read candidate can defend it as true, which moves the key from "Only one" to "Only two". The stem
also carries no "according to the chapter" hedge. The sentence is lifted from the Estimates Committee line ("the first Estimates Committee
in the post-independence era was constituted in 1950") and moved onto the PAC, which is what makes it slippery.
Fix: replace statement 4 with something false on any reading, for example "It was set up in 1964 on the recommendation of the Krishna
Menon Committee", and put "1921" in the explanation. Statement 2 ("Only the Parliament can take a final decision on its findings") stays true.

**M3. CS-POL-ch24-417 statement 1** ("The ministries/departments have, by and large, accepted their recommendations and initiated
action") is the book's evaluative claim (p.405) given as plain fact, in a stem with no "according to the chapter". The key "Only three"
depends on it being true. Real acceptance is mixed and contested. Add "According to the chapter" to the stem. The same sentence sits in
the LK-24.4 rule; write it as "the book says ministries have by and large accepted".

**M4. Unhedged chapter figures asserted as current fact.** The numbers match what I know of current practice (Lok Sabha Business Advisory
15 and Rules 15; Rajya Sabha Business Advisory 11 and Rules 16; House Committee 12/10; petitions, privileges, ethics and assurances 15/10),
but I could not verify the Rajya Sabha ones against a live source, and the stems do not say "according to the chapter": 512, 515, 517,
606, 607, 613, 616, 617, 620, 409 and 416 (st4). Add the hedge to those stems; it costs nothing and protects against a changed
strength. The Table 24.1 items 410 and 420 are already hedged. 401 says "at present" for 31 members; that is correct today (21+10).

**M5. Committee dates for the Lok Sabha only, stated without a House.** CS-POL-ch24-510 (st2) and 516 (st3) treat "the Committee on Papers
Laid on the Table was constituted in 1975" as true, and 506/510/516 treat 1953 as the date of Government Assurances and Subordinate
Legislation. As far as I recall, those are the Lok Sabha dates and the Rajya Sabha committees came later, so an unqualified statement is
fragile (this is memory, not a source I could open). The LK-24.5 rule repeats them flatly. Add "in the Lok Sabha" or "according to the
chapter" in the stems and the rule. The false variants ("1975" for Government Assurances) are safe either way.

**M6. CS-POL-ch24-618 (Statement-I/II, key "II explains I").** The book states the facts side by side (p.407) without a causal link. Taking
"II explains I" is reasonable, but "both correct, II does not explain I" is also defensible, since II only says what happens instead. Same
risk in 6.19-type items is absent. Either rewrite so II is plainly a restatement that cannot be argued either way, or change the pair to a
clearer relation.
