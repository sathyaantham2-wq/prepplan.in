## Author's least-sure facts, checked
- **Special majority-I includes approval of a national emergency (p.349): correct in real law.** Art 352(6) as amended by the
  44th Amendment needs a majority of the total membership of each House plus two-thirds of members present and voting. It is
  used only in the explanation of 717 (financial emergency is simple majority, Art 360, correct).
- **"PM and ministers' salaries not charged on the Consolidated Fund" (1105, 1115 st 4): real law agrees.** Art 112(3) lists the
  President, the presiding officers, debt charges, Supreme Court judges and High Court pensions, the CAG, court judgments and
  anything Parliament declares. Ministers' pay is voted (Salaries and Allowances of Ministers Act, 1952). The book adds the UPSC
  (Art 322) to its list, which is also true. No "false by omission" defect: the omission is the real position.
- **Art 109/110 money bill definition (1001, 1008, 1017): matches Art 110(1)-(2).** Fines, licence fees and local taxes are
  correctly the exceptions. Art 110(3) "Speaker's decision final": see M4 for the court-review caveat.
- **Joint sittings (905, 916): 3 times (1961, 1978, 2002) is right.** Bill years are the book's (1960, 1977, 2002). The three
  bills and the footnote facts (20-22, p.392) check out: Dowry (LS refused RS amendments), Banking Service Commission
  (Repeal) and POTA (LS passed, RS rejected). UPSC 2003 asked the same chain, key all three. No question puts the sitting
  year against the bill year.
- **Budget dates and terms:** 1 February since 2017, session start 31 January, railway budget merged 2017 (Acworth 1921,
  separated 1924), FRBM statements, Provisional Collection of Taxes Act 1931 (75 days), vote on account 2 months / one-sixth,
  longer in an election year, 24 DRSCs (1993, expanded 2004): all consistent with real law and with the book; none of these
  has moved since the book's edition (the 2026 budget was also presented 1 February).
- **State List 59 / Union 98 / Concurrent 52 (1202):** the 59 is arithmetically right (66 originally, five moved to the
  Concurrent List by the 42nd Amendment, entries 52 and 55 omitted by the 101st). Not a defect.
- **Art 117 / Art 108 details:** 108 (joint sitting; not money or Constitution amendment bills; more-than-four-days rule;
  lapse versus notified intention) and 117 (financial bills I/II, no recommendation needed for reduction or abolition of a tax)
  are correctly used in 913-915 and 1012-1014, except the book's own Art 117 line in M3.

## MEDIUM
**M1 - CS-POL-ch23-717 (how many, special majority-I), statement 2 "the impeachment of the President".** The key (Only one)
rests on the book putting impeachment under special majority-II (two-thirds of total membership). A reader reasoning from the
stem's own definition can say a two-thirds-of-total-membership vote also meets "majority of total membership AND two-thirds of
those present and voting", so the statement is arguably satisfied and Only two is defensible. The book's category is the only
reason it is false. Suggested: reword the stem to "which special majority does each require" (match style), or replace
statement 2 with a clean non-member such as the approval of a President's Rule (simple majority).

**M2 - CS-POL-ch23-1113 statement 2 (additional grant), key rests on the book's separation.** "An additional grant is made when
the amount authorised for a service is found insufficient." Art 115(1)(a) itself uses "supplementary or additional expenditure"
in one clause: insufficiency plus new service. So the statement is false only because Laxmikanth keeps a stricter distinction.
Key 1 and 4 only is the book's answer; 1 and 2 only is arguable under the constitutional text. Suggested: swap statement 2 for
"An additional grant covers a new service not contemplated in the budget" (true) and invert another, or replace by token grant.
(1120 is safe: it matches supplementary only.)

**M3 - CS-POL-ch23-1106 explanation repeats an inaccurate book line.** The explanation says "Parliament can reduce or abolish
a tax but cannot increase it (p.364)". Art 117 does not stop Parliament increasing a tax; it removes the President's
recommendation requirement for amendments reducing or abolishing a tax (the book says this correctly on p.361). The key is
unaffected (statement 4 is false either way). Suggested: explain as "a tax-increasing amendment needs the President's
recommendation; one that reduces or abolishes a tax does not (Art 117(2))".

**M4 - CS-POL-ch23-1005 and 1009 (Speaker's money-bill decision "cannot be questioned in any court").** That is Art 110(3)'s
text and the book's, so the keys are right for an Art-110 question. Currency note: in the Aadhaar judgment (2018) the minority
held the Speaker's certificate open to judicial review, and Rojer Mathew (2019) referred the question to a larger bench; a
careful aspirant may hesitate on 1009 statement 2 ("can be questioned in a court of law", false). Add a line to the
explanations that the text makes the decision final and that its reviewability was referred to a larger bench.

**M5 - CS-POL-ch23-1004 weak distractors.** Options C ("A money bill") and D ("A bill containing exclusively the matters mentioned
in Article 110") are the same thing, so one is wasted (a candidate who rejects one rejects the other). Replace D with e.g. "A
Constitution amendment bill".
