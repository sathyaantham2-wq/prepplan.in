# Review of lk05.json (Laxmikanth ch5, Preamble) - independent

Verdict: **fail (revise)**. Counts: HIGH 3 (2 item-level, 1 systemic), MEDIUM 10, LOW 6.
All 120 questions were answered from ch05.txt (pdf pp.85-92) before reading the key. Every key is factually
right and every cited page contains its fact. The HIGHs are about answer-guessability and Statement-I/II logic,
not wrong facts. Preamble text is unchanged since 1976, so nothing has gone out of date after any exam year.

## HIGH
**H1 (systemic) - the "which statements" keys can be guessed from the option pattern, and the author's own report understates it.**
Findings from the script:
- 22 of the 29 three-statement items have an identical option set {1 and 2 only, 1 only, 1, 2 and 3, 2 only}.
  The key is always "1 and 2 only", and the false statement is always statement 3. Items:
  110, 207, 208, 210, 213, 306, 308, 309, 310, 312, 406, 409, 410, 412, 413, 507, 510, 512, 514, 606, 608, 612.
- Choosing "1 and 2 only" with no knowledge scores 22/29 on three-statement items.
- Across all 54 "which" items, the false statement sits at the last or second-last position in 45. It is never at
  position 2 of a four-statement item. In four-statement items the two options with three statements are
  "all but last" or "all but 3rd", and the key is one of them in 23 of 25.
- No "which" item has all statements correct, and none has statement 2 alone false among four.
- The false statement is usually the absolute or extreme one ("alone", "licence", "negative concept",
  "limits sovereignty", "reserved for a privileged class"), so a reader spots it without knowing the chapter.
Fix: vary the position of the false statement (1, 2 and 3 equally); include keys such as "2 and 3 only",
"1 and 3 only", "3 only" and "all correct"; build option sets from the real statement combinations rather than
one template; make false statements plausible swaps of terms, not absurd extremes.

**CS-POL-ch05-118 (Statement-I/II) - two defensible relations.**
I: the Preamble as adopted did not contain Socialist, Secular, Integrity. II: the 42nd Amendment added them.
II is a later event that follows from I. It does not give a reason why the words were absent in 1949. A student
can defend "both correct, II does not explain I", but the key says "explains". Rewrite, e.g. I: "The Preamble
today contains the word Integrity" with II: "the 42nd Amendment added it" (now a clean "explains"), or make II an
independent fact.

**CS-POL-ch05-418 (Statement-I/II) - II restates I, so the relation is arguable.**
I: liberty is not absolute but qualified. II: liberty must be enjoyed within the limits in the Constitution.
These are the same proposition in two wordings. "Explains" and "does not explain" are both defensible. Replace II
with a genuine reason (e.g. that Fundamental Rights carry restrictions) or change the pair.

## MEDIUM
- **318**: I: "democratic" covers social and economic democracy. II: Ambedkar's quote on social democracy. The book
  links them ("this dimension was stressed by"), so "explains" is the likelier key. But II is a speaker's
  opinion, so the relation is contestable. Strengthen or reword.
- **110**: statements 1 and 3 contradict each other, so exactly one of them is false. That is a reading trick.
  The statement 2 padding ("opens with We, the People") is not tied to the topic.
- **311, 313, 416, 412**: false statements are absurd ("public offices reserved for a privileged class",
  "discrimination permitted", "liberty is absolute / licence", "enjoyed without any limitations"). The
  distractors test nothing. Use plausible near-misses.
- **106 (W list)**: the first and third distractor explanations are the same sentence. Option-level feedback is
  wrong for one option.
- **604, 605**: all three wrong-option explanations in 604 are the same sentence ("nothing but a revolution can
  alter it"), and the 605 entries do not map to their options. Rewrite per option.
- **119, 219, 319, 519, 619 (AR, Statement-I incorrect)**: the two "both correct" explanations are word-for-word
  identical in each item. Differentiate "explains" from "does not explain" in the feedback.
- **111 and 610**: near-identical content (enactment of the Preamble: after the rest, President's motion, SC view
  consistent with founders). One should be replaced. Smaller overlaps: 115 with 102; 209 with 217; 107 with 120.
- **205**: pg lists [86, 88, 89], but the fact (Articles 25-28, SC 1974) is only on p.86. The extra pages exist
  only to support distractors. The trap label "close numbers" is misleading because the options are article ranges.
- **203, 211, 220 (Sri Lanka as theocratic)**: this is the footnote 9 classification of Laxmikanth, not a settled
  fact. It is acceptable in 203 ("cited in the chapter"), but 211 statement 4 and 220 state it as fact.
  Add "as cited in the chapter" or avoid Sri Lanka.
- **Format mix** against the 2020-26 column of the trends doc (26% direct / 48% which / 12% how-many / 6% Statement-I/II):
  pack = 25% direct (30), 45% which (54), 15% how-many (18), 10% Statement-I/II (12), 5% match (6). This is on target.
  Statement-I/II is high but acceptable. The weak point is the quality of the "which" keys (H1), not the mix.
  There is no case-study item (3% in the trends doc), which is fine.
