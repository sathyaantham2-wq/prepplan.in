## HIGH
**H1 - CS-POL-ch02-1114 (LK-2.11 #14), wrong key / unsupported fact.** Statement 2 says "Article 394 (commencement) was brought
into force on 26 January 1950 with the rest"; the key ("1 and 2 only") treats it as true. The chapter (p.45) lists the articles in
force on 26 Nov 1949 as 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391, 392 and 393 and never says anything about Article 394.
In the Constitution itself Article 394 ("This article and Articles 5, 6, ... 393 shall come into force at once") took effect on 26
November 1949. So statement 2 is false in law and merely inferred by omission from the book; the correct key is "1 only". The
explanation ("the remaining provisions came into force on 26 January 1950") asserts something p.45 does not say about 394.
Fix: drop statement 2, or replace with a statement the page does state (for example Article 60 in force on 26 Nov 1949 and Article
366 in force on 26 Nov 1949), and re-key.

**H2 - CS-POL-ch02-1010 (LK-2.10 #10), second defensible answer.** Key "2, 3 and 4 only" needs statement 2 ("the members signed the
Constitution that day", 24 January 1950) to be true. That rests on the Table 2.6 note (p.53). But p.44 says the Constitution
"received the signatures of the members and the president" on 26 November 1949 and that 284 members "signed" that day, and item
1013 statement 2 is keyed true on exactly that reading. A student who follows p.44 marks statement 2 false and the pack offers her
"3 and 4 only" as a live option. Statement 1 ("the meeting of 24 January 1950 was the Eleventh Session") is also arguable against
p.42 ("held its final session" on 24 January 1950). The stem does not anchor to the Table 2.6 note the way 1004 does.
Fix: remove statement 2 from 1010 (keep 3 and 4 from p.42) and replace statement 1 with a cleaner one, e.g. "The Eleventh Session
ended on 24 January 1950" (false, p.53 table).
