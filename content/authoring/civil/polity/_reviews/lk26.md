## HIGH
**H1 - CS-POL-ch26-512 (Statement 2: American Supreme Court's appellate jurisdiction "is confined to constitutional cases only").**
The key `2 and 3 only` needs statement 2 true. It is false in real law: the US Supreme Court hears appeals on federal
statutes and treaties, on admiralty, and on diversity matters via certiorari (Art III s.2 gives it appellate jurisdiction
over all federal-law and diversity cases). The stem says "comparing the appellate jurisdiction of the Indian and American
Supreme Courts", not "according to Table 26.1". A student who knows US law picks `3 only`. This is the recurring defect
(the book's own table is loose). Fix: begin the stem "According to Table 26.1 of the chapter, consider ..." and keep the
rest, or replace statement 2 with a fact true in law (the Indian Court has advisory jurisdiction, the American has none,
which is true; or special leave).

**H2 - CS-POL-ch26-716 (statement 2: the American Supreme Court's jurisdiction and powers "can be enlarged by the Congress").**
Key `Only two` treats statement 2 as false (the book: American jurisdiction "limited to that conferred by the
Constitution"). In law Congress sets the Court's size and, under Art III s.2, regulates and may extend its appellate
jurisdiction by statute (Judiciary Act 1925 and later); only the constitutional original jurisdiction cannot be
enlarged (Marbury). So "Only three" is defensible. Same fix as H1: attribute the stem to Table 26.1, or reword
statement 2 to the original jurisdiction ("The original jurisdiction of the American Supreme Court can be enlarged by
Congress") which is false in law and in the book. Statement 4 (US Court has no superintendence over state courts) is sound.
