## HIGH
1. **CS-POL-ch01-706, statement 1** ("special representation to women and zamindars (landlords)"). Book p.33 says "women and labour (workers)". Key marks statement 1 false, giving "2 and 3 only". Historically the 1935 Act did give landholders (zamindars) reserved seats in the provincial legislatures, so a well-read aspirant can defend statement 1 as true and pick "1, 2 and 3". Not a book-only falsifier. Fix: swap in a clearly false thing (for example "women and the clergy") or ask about labour only.
2. **CS-POL-ch01-1316, statement 1** ("known as the Act for the *Better* Government of India"). The book calls it "Act for the *Good* Government of India" (p.29), and the pack keys it false, giving "None". The statute's real long title is "An Act for the Better Government of India", so statement 1 is true historically. Defensible answer is "Only one". Fix: use a different falsifier, such as a wrong body abolished. Do not use Better/Good.

2. **1018** (S-I Mountbatten first Governor-General, S-II he put forth the plan as Viceroy, key "does not explain"). The book draws no causal link, but a candidate can argue that being the Viceroy who drew the plan explains why he took the first post. Judgement call, not a book fact. Replace S-II with a fact that is plainly unrelated or plainly false.
3. **1119** (Baldev Singh Defence, Bhabha Works). These are two unrelated table lookups with the key "does not explain". It tests no reasoning, and 818 uses the same shape. Rewrite as a true/false pair.
4. **1019 and 1007.2 / 910.2** (the Governor-General "could act at his discretion"). The key relies on p.35 item 11 ("advice ... in all matters"), but item 10 gives the Governor-General the reserved veto and full power to assent. A reader can see a partly supported "discretion". Keep it, but word it as "could act against the advice of his ministers in matters other than veto".
5. **1307.1 and 1318 S-II** ("Crown rule continued until the Constitution came into being on 26 January 1950"). It is false per p.25, but historically the Crown stayed head of state of the dominion until 1950. It uses the book's own phrase "Crown rule", so it is fair per the book, though a sharp reader may object. Safer: "until 15 August 1948".
6. **707.3 and 719 S-I** (the Joint Public Service Commission for the federation and the princely states). The book says "two or more provinces". The 1935 Act also let federated states use the federal commission by agreement, so the falsifier is not clean. Use a different wrong partner, such as "the provinces and the Viceroy".
7. **"Republics" falsifier used three times** (912.3, 1015.1, 1017.1). It is correct, since the book says Dominions, but it is the same trick each time and is easy to spot. Vary it.
8. **Weak or generic distractor rationales.** 805 w gives "Both accepted it" twice; 903, 904 and 1005 are generic.
