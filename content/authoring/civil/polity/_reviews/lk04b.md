## HIGH
**H1 - option structure gives away the key in 28 of 38 four-statement "which" items.** In these items the option set is
the key (exactly three statements), "1, 2, 3 and 4", and two pairs (or in 8 items three pairs plus the key). The key is the
only option with three statements, so a student who knows no fact and rejects "all four" and the two same-sized pairs
scores 28/38 = 74% (chance 25%). Because the rule is mechanical it also defeats the "reject one wrong statement"
skill the trends doc says the exam rewards. Same pattern as the round-1 H1 in lk05. Fix: in about half of these, make
two options three-statement combinations (for example key 1,3,4 beside 1,2,4 and 2,3,4) and make "all four" a key now
and then (currently 0 of 38 four-statement "which" items has "1, 2, 3 and 4" as the key; one "how many" has "All four").
Ids (CS-POL-ch04-): 707, 710, 712, 713, 808, 810, 813, 907, 908, 911, 914, 1007, 1009, 1010, 1012, 1107, 1109, 1110,
1112, 1207, 1210, 1212, 1213, 1307, 1308, 1310, 1312, 1313.
(Key shapes: 4-statement keys are "1,2,3 only" x7, "1,2,4 only" x7, "1,3,4 only" x7, "2,3,4 only" x7, so every
three-statement combination is balanced; the leak is the option-size shape, not the combination.)

## MEDIUM
**M1 - 713 statement 3 rests on a book generalisation.** "Part VII is the only Part that amendments have deleted so far."
p.62 does say the amendments "have deleted ... one Part (VII)", but the stem does not say "according to the chapter", and
in the actual Constitution the original Part IX (Territories in Part D) was also removed before the 73rd Amendment
inserted a new Part IX. A student who knows this can reject statement 3 and pick "1 and 2 only", which is an option.
Fix: attribute the statement ("According to Laxmikanth, the amendments since 1951 have deleted one Part, namely...") or
replace it with "Part VII dealt with the States in Part B of the First Schedule".

**M2 - concepts LK-4.7 and LK-4.8 (40 items) are pure Article-range lookup.** "Part XIX covers 361 to 367", "Chapter II of
Part XII covers 292 to 293", "Part XVII chapter III covers 348 to 349". No reasoning, and range arithmetic of this kind has
almost no exam record (the tagged PYQs for ch4 are about sources, Schedules, rule of law, Part count). Keep the Part-to-subject
items (801, 804, 805, 820, 701, 704, 705) and the Part-boundary reasoning items (104, 112, 903, 009, 003, 103), and replace
about a third of the range-only statements with tested content (for example 801/802 type, amendments that added Parts,
number of Parts and Schedules, Articles that moved Parts).

**M4 - 318 "II explains I" is arguable.** I: the 1935 Act is the most profound influence. II: more than half of the
provisions resemble the 1935 Act. II is the measure of I rather than a separate reason, so "correct but does not explain"
is partly defensible. Same shape, weaker: 118 (Centre all-powerful vs federal-to-unitary conversion; also I says "National
Emergency" while the book says "an emergency"), 918 (direct access to the Supreme Court vs Article 32 remedies), 718
(Part VII absent vs deleted by the 7th Amendment). 718 and 918 are fair (II is the legal cause); 118 and 318 are the soft ones.
Make II a distinct reason or make the pair independent.

**M5 - empty explanations for correct statements.** Every "which" and "how many" explanation reads "Statement 2 is correct:

carry a fix. Add the fact in one clause. Also 719 and 819 list the same "Rejected: ... Statement-I as correct" text three
times in "w", and 1212 has a doubled ".;" in "w".

**M6 - 312 and 308 contain two true "source" statements that look contradictory.** 308 statements 1 and 2: suspension of
