## HIGH (answer defensibility / item cannot be answered as stated)

1. **C7M-8.6 Q19 (cloth merchant, scarf 3/4 m ₹120, curtain 2 1/2 m ₹450).** Stem says "He wants to earn as much as possible from the 12 m", then lists only two plans. 3 curtains + 6 scarves (7.5 m + 4.5 m, exactly 12 m) earns ₹2,070, more than the keyed best plan (4 curtains + 2 scarves, ₹2,040). Computed over k = 0..4 curtains: 1,920 / 1,890 / 1,980 / 2,070 / 2,040. A sharp student can fairly say the key's "second plan earns ₹120 more" is not the maximum. Fix: replace "as much as possible" with "He compares two plans: scarves only, or the greatest number of whole curtains first and scarves from what is left", or make the key's (d) also note the 3-curtain plan.
2. **C7M-8.6 Q18 (lemon juice, cups 1/5, 1/3, 1/2 L).** Part (d) turns on "the volunteers can fill only a limited number of cups" but no number is given; the key invents "about 30 cups". Part (d) cannot be decided from the stem (QUESTION_STANDARD s11: state everything the item needs). Fix: state the limit, e.g. "at most 30 cups", so the key (27 cups, ₹243) is derivable.

5. **C7M-8.2 Q10 and Q11: row/column convention.** The book's rule (p.181): rows = denominator of the multiplicand, columns = denominator of the multiplier, with multiplier written first (3/4 x 2/5 gives 5 rows, 4 columns). Q11 key "20 rows and 15 columns" for 1/15 x 1/20 follows this, but the stem never states the convention, so "15 rows and 20 columns" is a defensible reading. Q10 says "6 rows and 5 columns" for 4/6 x 3/5, which is the reverse of the book (that would be 5 rows, 6 columns). Fix: in Q11 ask only for the total number of parts (300) or state the convention; in Q10 say "5 rows and 6 columns" or "a unit square with 6 and 5 equal strips".
6. **C7M-8.3 Q20, key (d):** "Rani gets nearly double the time" but 5/6 is exactly double 5/12. Fix to "exactly double".
7. **C7M-8.4 Q17 (200 g sugar, 9/10 each step).** Key (d) "still a positive quantity" does not prove it never reaches 0 (that needs the idea that a positive number times 9/10 is positive), and part (c) is stated as decimal 145.8 g. Decimals in fraction problems are scope_out ("decimals, percentages, ratio and proportion in fraction problems"). Fix: give the final answer as 729/5 g only, and key (d) as "each product of two positive fractions is positive, so it never becomes 0".
8. **C7M-8.4 Q18 (cashew prices):** answers ₹76.80 and ₹86.40 are decimals (scope_out line above). Change numbers so they are whole rupees, e.g. ₹100 per 100 g: 120, then 96; clearance 9/10 gives 108; condition "not below ₹100". Also the decision is forced (only 9/10 works), not a trade-off.
