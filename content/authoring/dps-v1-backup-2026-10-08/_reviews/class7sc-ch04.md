# Review: DPS pack, Class 7 Science, ch04 "The World of Metals and Non-metals" (gecu104)

File: content/authoring/dps/class7sc/ch04.json. Questions read: 131 of 131 (every item, every case-study part, every step list). Facts checked against content/extracted/gecu104/pages/ (all 16 pages). Arithmetic recomputed in code (4.2 #12: 5 x 2 = 10 km and 250 m = 0.25 km, 0.25/2 = 0.125 g; 4.2 #15: 20/2 = 10 g, not 2 g; all correct). Step marks sum to `m` in every item (checked in code).

Overall: keys are sound (no wrong key found) and facts match the book, but the chapter is TEMPLATED and thin on real reasoning. Every concept has the same slot order and the same frames, several case studies have a strawman decision or a free part (a), and many 5-mark items are "describe the book's activity". Not yet at the section-9 standard; fix the items below.

## HIGH

1. C7SC-4.6 case study #22 (eco club scrap drive). Part (a) "Which two metals does the chapter name as being recycled in India?" is printed in the stem: "The club leader says that metals, especially iron and aluminium, are recycled in India." Free mark. Fix: delete that sentence from the stem (or turn (a) into a question the stem does not answer, e.g. why a drive for foil trays and rods makes sense).

2. C7SC-4.5 case study #22 (elements X, Y, Z). Key part (b) says "Burning Y makes sulfur dioxide", but the stem never names Y: it says only "a yellow solid". The key uses an identity the student is not given (and "yellow" is not in the book). A correct answer ("its oxide dissolves to form an acid; Y itself does not react with water") would be marked against a key that assumes sulfur. Fix: write "Element Y, sulfur" in the stem, or rewrite key (b) to "burning Y forms an acidic oxide that dissolves in water; Y itself does not react with water". Also part (d) "Name one more property you would test" is a recall step, not a decision (see 6).

## MEDIUM

3. Strawman decisions. The last part is not a real trade-off in these case studies; the "wrong" side is absurd:
   - 4.2 #21 (bridge model): Group B wants ropes of coal strips, Group C says "only gold" can be drawn into wires, and (d) "which group's plan should the school accept?" has the obvious answer (steel). Fix: give two workable options (e.g. a steel wire rope vs a copper wire rope with data on cost or load that the stem states) and ask which to choose and what the book lets you say.
   - 4.2 #20 (cousin's project): (d) "choose a material for a musical string" from coal, sulfur and a coin. Fix: choose between two metals (copper vs aluminium wire), justified by what the book says both do.
   - 4.1 #21 (science fair): visitor says "Metals are always hard and solid"; (c) is "should the sign be put up" and (b) has already refuted it. Fix: make the choice between two proposed signs, one of which is qualified.
   - 4.3 #22 (screwdrivers): metal-foil gloves; model A with a metal handle vs B with plastic. Both choices are obvious. Fix: compare two real options, e.g. rubber vs cloth gloves, with reasons from poor conduction.
   - 4.5 #23 (sodium in water): "keep sodium in water to save kerosene" and (d) "All metals and non-metals must be stored under a liquid". Both are absurd. Fix: ask which of sodium, phosphorus and magnesium needs a liquid and why (magnesium does not in the book).
   - 4.6 #21 (health camp): (d) "choose between a steel pan and an iodine solution bottle for cooking and for first aid". Not a decision. Fix: choose the best non-metal or alloy for each of two jobs where two answers compete.
   - 4.6 #22 (d) "Should the poster say metals matter only for utensils and tools?" is a strawman on the word "only".
   - 4.6 #16 (Dev: non-metals are of no use) and 4.4 claim items are in the same mould (see 5).

4. Free or recall-only early parts in case studies (the "case study" is a four-part recall quiz):
   - 4.6 #21: (a) chlorine is already in the stem ("the health worker adds chlorine" to well water), (b) iodine solution is in the stem, (d) steel are "alloys" in the stem. Rewrite so the stem gives situation only, parts need the book.
   - 4.4 #21: (a) "which nail" is restated in the stem ("Only one nail shows a brown deposit"), (b) and (c) just read back the stem. Parts do not climb. Fix: ask (b) and (c) together as a reason, move a new situation (e.g. same nail, boiled water but no oil) to (c).
   - 4.1 #21 (a): "Which exhibit shows malleability, and what property of its metal makes it possible?" is circular (the property is malleability, named in the question). Ask "name the property that lets silver be made into foil".
   - 4.2 #20 (a) and (c), 4.3 #21 (a)-(b), 4.4 #22 (a)-(c): one-fact recall each; only the last part uses reasoning.

5. All seven claim-checks are keyed the same way: the claim is wrong ("No / the claim is wrong"). Items: 4.1 #15 and #16, 4.2 #15, 4.3 #16, 4.4 #16, 4.5 #17, 4.6 #16. Students learn "disagree". Fix: make at least two correct-in-a-limited-case or correct claims (e.g. "Meena says a metal spoon in hot water will get hotter at its top than a wooden one in the same time: agree?" keyed Yes with a reason; or "a nail in oil alone will not rust: right, but only if air and water are both kept out"). Several claims are strawmen: 4.6 #16 "no use in daily life", 4.2 #16 "string out of coal", 4.2 #15 (slip in a division). 4.3 #16 (Priya: copper vessels boil water "because copper is a good conductor of electricity") reuses the book's own Exercise 3(iv) almost word for word.

(b) ...", claim-check, show-it-cannot-work ("A student says Y will happen. Show why this cannot"), reverse ("Write a situation..."), 5-mark "Describe Activity 4.n", 5-mark compare, two case studies.

Standard demands the false statement spread over 1, 2, 3 and the keyed combination vary ("1 only", "2 and 3 only", "1 and 3 only", "all three"). Fix: re-key at least three of them (e.g. false = statement 1 in 4.2, statement 2 in 4.3 and 4.6, none false in 4.4 with key "1, 2 and 3").
   - Assertion-reason: 4.2, 4.3, 4.4, 4.5 and 4.6 (#8/#9) each use the pair "R explains A", then "R true but not explaining A". Vary: use a case with A false (only one such, 4.6 #10), one where R is false, one with a reversed order.

10. 4.3 case #21 (d): "From aluminium, wood and stone, which should the pan body and which the handle be made of?" Key says wood for the handle, but the stem also offers stone, a poor conductor of electricity in the book (Table 4.2); the book never says whether stone is a good or poor conductor of heat. Two defensible answers for the handle. Fix: replace stone by copper or iron (a metal) so only wood is a poor conductor of heat.

11. 4.6 short answer #12: "(b) Why would a school's first-aid box and its water tank both depend on non-metals?" The key just repeats (a). Per section 11, (b) must be a consequence or reason.
