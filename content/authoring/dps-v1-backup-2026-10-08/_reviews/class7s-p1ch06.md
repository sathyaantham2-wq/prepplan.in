# Review: DPS bank, Class 7 Social Science, Part I ch 6 "The Age of Reorganisation" (gees106)

File: `content/authoring/dps/class7s/p1ch06.json`. Read: all 144 questions (7 concepts, 20-21 each), no sampling.
Checked against the extracted pages 117-143 (`content/extracted/gees106/pages/`), the scope record
`content/authoring/class7s/p1ch06.json`, `docs/QUESTION_STANDARD.md` and the DPS reference
`6._The_age_of_reorganisation.pdf.txt`. Every number recomputed by hand and re-checked (see end).

Headline: no wrong key and no arithmetic error. Facts are faithful to this book almost everywhere. The
problems are (1) two items whose key is not safely unique, (2) one near-verbatim copy of a DPS item and
two more that mirror one, (3) case studies where one part is a free mark from the stem or the decision is
a strawman, and (4) a very strong template: every concept has the same slot layout and the same
reasoning moves. As a bank it is correct but reads as one pattern repeated seven times.

## HIGH

1. **C7S-6.1 #11 (short_answer, 2m)** "(a) Name two dynasties from within the subcontinent and two from
   outside it ... (b) Which one of your outside dynasties came originally from central Asia, and in which
   century CE did it probably enter India?" The student chooses the outside dynasties, so a student who
   names Indo-Greeks and Shakas has no valid answer to (b). The book gives a central-Asian origin only
   for the Kushanas (p.136). The key says "any correct pairs" yet step 2 insists on Kushanas.
   Fix: "(a) Name two dynasties from within ... and the Kushanas, Indo-Greeks and Shakas are the outsiders;
   (b) Which of these came originally from central Asia ..." or require Kushanas to be among the two named.

2. **C7S-6.5 #4 (scenario mcq)** "A Roman merchant in the Sangam Age wants spices, timber and ivory ...
   Which kingdom's kings would have been his most natural trade partners?" Key: Cheras. But the book also
   says the Satavahanas traded spices and ivory with the Roman Empire (p.125), and the Pandyas traded with
   Greeks and Romans (p.133). Only "timber" singles out the Cheras, and "most natural" is a judgement.
   A student who argues Satavahana (spices, ivory, Roman trade) has a defensible reading.
   Fix: ask directly, "Which kingdom does the chapter describe as a hub for the export of spices, timber,
   ivory and pearls?" or make timber and pearls both part of the stem.

3. **C7S-6.7 #17 (long_answer 5m)** "Evaluate the contribution of the Indo-Greeks, the Shakas and the
   Kushanas to Indian culture and society, using the chapter." This is the DPS Q4 ("Evaluate the
   contribution of the foreign-origin dynasties like the Indo-Greeks, Sakas, and Kushanas to Indian
   culture and society") almost word for word. Standard section 5: never copy a school item.
   Fix: change the shape, e.g. "Choose the one of these three peoples whose arrival changed the
   subcontinent's art the most and defend your choice with two pieces of evidence, and say what
   the other two added." Also see MEDIUM 12 and 13 (two more DPS-frame items).

## MEDIUM

4. **C7S-6.4 #19 (case study) part (c)** "Which southern kingdom supplied the pearls in note (iii)?" The
   stem already says note (iii) was found "in the section on the Pandya kingdom". Free mark.
   Fix: drop "in the section on the Pandya kingdom" from the stem and give only "he states that he gets
   hundreds of pearls brought from a southern kingdom".

5. **C7S-6.2 #19 (case study) part (a)** "By about which year BCE was the Shunga empire gone?" The stem
   gives both numbers (took power around 185 BCE; gone a century later), so (a) is pure arithmetic and
   needs no chapter. Fix: stem says only "around 185 BCE", and (a) becomes "How long, according to the
   chapter, did the Shunga empire last after him, and so by about which year BCE was it gone?"

6. **C7S-6.1 #20 (case study) part (a)** The stem says "fight for the district" and "offer his
   daughter ... marriage"; (a) only asks for the book's names for these. Mostly given away. Also the stem
   never says what Plan 2 does about the border district (only that he gives up "one small border
   village"), so the trade-off in (d) is unclear. Fix: describe Plan 1 as "send his army" and Plan 2
   as "arrange a family link with the neighbour", state what Plan 2 secures, and say the amounts are in
   rupees (they have no unit now).

7. **Strawman or lopsided decisions (part d).**
   - C7S-6.2 #19: 'Builder of a lasting empire' vs 'Defender of a smaller empire'. The chapter says
     "smaller" and "did not last", so one title is already refuted; not a real trade-off.
   - C7S-6.7 #20: caption 'The Kushanas controlled the Silk Route' vs 'held significant sections'.
     The key itself says the second fits; a strawman. Fix: offer two captions that are both partly
     supported, or ask what extra figure would justify the first.
   - C7S-6.6 #18: "which note would you leave out" - any note works and the answer is word arithmetic;
     the decision does not use the chapter.
   - C7S-6.7 #19 (d): A and D are the only sensible pair; "fusion" is the Gandhara example in the book.
   - C7S-6.1 #19 (d): strip "in order they rose" vs grouped. The book gives no order of rise in text
     (only Fig. 6.28), so the first option cannot be argued from the chapter.
   - C7S-6.3 #20 (d), 6.2 #18 (d), 6.3 #19 (d): "either is acceptable" with no data pointing either way.
   Good ones to keep as models: C7S-6.5 #20 (cost 70 crore vs 10% more land), 6.4 #19 (d) (conflict vs
   contact), 6.6 #19 (d) (60% Greek deity vs book's statement).

8. **Claim-checks are all the same move.** C7S-6.1 #14, 6.2 #13, 6.3 #14, 6.4 #13, 6.5 #14, 6.6 #13,
   6.7 #14: "<Name> says 'X happened, so Y must be true'. Do you agree?" Every verdict is "No", every claim
   is an absolute built to fail, several are absurd (6.3 #14 "queens counted for nothing"). Students learn
   the cue. Fix: make two or three of them partly right ("agree, but only for ..."), and vary the frame
   (a table to read, a date to test, two students disagree).

9. **Case-study part (a)/(b) on pure recall plus a side calculation.** Almost every case study is: (a)
   name, (b) name or sum, (c) recall or sum, (d) "either is acceptable". The arithmetic (rupees, minutes,
   percentages, word counts) is sound but invented around the chapter rather than needing it. Fix: keep
   them, but make at least one part per study turn on a chapter idea (a date comparison, a who-did-what
   sequence, a source-reading), as 6.5 #20 and 6.4 #19 do.

10. **Figure-dependent items.** C7S-6.2 #11 ("the collage of Shunga art (Fig. 6.6)") and #12 ("carvings on the
    Bharhut railings") can be answered only from the book's figure captions; neither tag is `figure` and
    the stem states nothing. Fix: list the objects in the stem or drop the item and report it.
    (scope_out says "Map exercises on approximate borders - Not answerable without the map"; no map item
    was found, so nothing violates it.)

11. **Repeated content within a concept (near-duplicates, wasted cells).**
    - 6.1: inside/outside sorting appears in #7, #11, #16, #18 and #19.
    - 6.4: "location unknown, did not invade the south" appears in #5, #12, #14, #17 and #19(a).
    - 6.6: Shaka-year arithmetic appears in #5, #11, #14 and #17.
    - 6.7: Gandhara vs Mathura (stone, influence, look) in #1, #4, #8, #11, #15, #18, #19.
    Fix: keep one or two of each and replace the rest with other ideas from the concept.

12. **C7S-6.3 #18 (5m)** "Compare the Shungas and the Satavahanas with respect to the area they ruled,
    ..., their patronage, and how their rule ended." This is DPS Q1 ("Compare the rule of the Shungas
    with that of the Satavahanas with respect to the areas ruled, ... belief systems") with two axes
    swapped. Fix: change the pair or the question (e.g. which of the two left the better record of
    trade, using three pieces of evidence).

13. **C7S-6.5 #18 (5m)** Compare Cholas, Cheras, Pandyas by region, capital, known-for mirrors DPS Q3;
    **C7S-6.4 #16/#17** (inscription tells about Kharavela) mirror DPS Q2. Same fix: change the
    frame so it is not a restatement of a school question.

14. **Claims beyond this book.**
    - C7S-6.5 #18 key: Pandyas "southern Tamil Nadu" and Cholas "(Kaveri delta)": the book says "parts
      of Tamil Nadu and the surrounding regions" and "parts of south India". Say what the book says.
    - C7S-6.7 #17 key: "The Shakas developed the Shaka Samvat": the book says it was developed "during
      this period" (p.135); 6.6 #17 words it correctly. Make them agree.
    - C7S-6.4 #14 step 1 "the king wrote the record about himself, so it may praise him": source
      criticism the chapter does not teach (it only says the battle's location is unknown). Accept it as
      a reasoning mark only if the stem states it, or drop that step.
    - C7S-6.3 #9 and #19: "exports" and "imports" are inferred. The book says goods "exchanged" were spices,
      textiles, sandalwood, gold-plated pearls, ivory and "Imports included glass and perfumed ointments".
      Accept it, but note the sort is an inference.
    - C7S-6.3 #11: "Why were the Naneghat caves built close to a trade route?" The book says only that they
      were located close to one and "used for" tolls and rest; it does not give a reason for building.
      Fix: "What were the Naneghat caves used for?"
    - C7S-6.3 #12 key: "professions were not as fixed as we may think" is the book's open THINK ABOUT IT, not a
      stated fact; accept any reasonable inference and say so in the key.
    - C7S-6.6 #3 option 3 ("Developed by the Kushana king Kanishka"): the book does not attribute the
      calendar to anyone; some historians do link it to Kanishka. Replace with a clearly false option.
    - C7S-6.6 #7 statement 3 "Official ... publications give dates in both eras" generalises from one
      publication (Fig. 6.21). Use "An official Government of India publication shown in the chapter".

15. **Explanations that point to a shuffled option by position** (loader shuffles options): C7S-6.2 #4
    ("the third reply is wrong"), 6.4 #2 ("the last option"), 6.4 #5 ("the second option is also false").
    Fix: name the option's content instead.

## LOW

16. **Template across concepts.** Every concept has the same layout: indices 0-2 plain mcq, 3 a statement-style
    mcq, 4-5 scenario mcq, 6 assertion-reason, 7 multi_statement, (match on odd concepts), then eight
    short answers in the same order (recall, (a)/(b) recall, (a)/(b), ..., claim-check at slot 14, a
    sorted/"how can historians know" item, "Justify this statement" 5m, "Compare/Explain" 5m, two case
    studies of (a) (b) (c) (d)). 11 of 14 case studies end "Either is acceptable if justified". The
    5-mark pair is always "'<quotation>.' Justify" plus "Compare/Explain ... cover X, Y, Z, W". This is
    a pattern with swapped nouns: the multi_statement and assertion-reason keys do vary well (false
    statement in 1, 2, 3 and "none" across the chapter; AR verdicts rotate), but the surrounding
    shape does not. Vary at least the stems and the order of parts from concept to concept.

17. **Level labels inflated.** Many items labelled Analyse/Evaluate are retrieval: 6.4 #8, 6.5 #9, 6.6 #8,
    6.7 #9, #11, #13 are one-fact lookups with two parts, and (b) of several (a)/(b) items is a second
    fact, not a consequence (standard section 11: "an (a)/(b) item is not two recall facts"). Chapter
    mix by label is 41% Remember/Understand and 44% Analyse/Evaluate/Create, so on paper it passes, but the
    true reasoning share is nearer a third.

18. **Some distractors are invented, not chapter confusions.** C7S-6.5 #1 (rules of marriage alliances, lists of
    tolls), 6.7 #5 (copied Satavahana coins, ruled only the Shakas), 6.6 #4 ("Alexander's army carried
    Indian deities home"; "Shakas took over a Greek mint"), 6.3 #0 ("Yavanas" is not in the chapter),
    6.2 #1 (the headless statue "at the stupa"). Use the chapter's look-alikes (Kharavela vs Karikala,
    Gandhara vs Mathura, Naneghat vs Karla).

19. **Option form tells.** 6.2 #1 correct option is the only one without "They"; 6.2 #4 correct option is
    the only one not starting "Correct/Wrong"; 6.4 #2 the correct option is the only real quotation from

    not systematically the longest (it is longest in only 5 of 42 and 4 of those are equal-length ties):
    this requirement is met.

20. **`rev` flags inconsistent.** Marked: 6.3 #2, 6.7 #2. Not marked, though statements turn on
    "No new kingdoms", "played no part", "held no part", "All ... only": 6.1 #3, 6.3 #7, 6.6 #7, 6.7 #7,
    6.2 #6 (R "refused"). Mark all or none.

21. **Small wording.** 6.3 #9 "Whose farming, in which river system" - "whose" has no answer in the key; 6.2 #15
    "compare ... how long it lasted" - the chapter gives no duration for the Maurya empire, so only the
