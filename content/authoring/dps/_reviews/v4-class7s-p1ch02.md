- 2.5 #20 (5 marks). "The pointer of a wind vane at Town X turns to face Town Y ... (i) the air is moving from X towards Y; (ii) X has the higher pressure." The book says only "the pointer turns in the direction of the wind" and, elsewhere, that farmers use the direction "to predict where rain might come from", i.e. it uses direction both as "towards" and "from". By the usual meteorological convention a vane points INTO the wind (the wind comes from Y), which reverses (i) and (ii). A strong student can defend a second answer. Smallest fix: state the movement in the stem ("the wind is blowing from Town X towards Town Y") and delete (i), or make (i) a different step (which instrument shows direction); keep (ii)-(v). Same wording risk, lower stakes, in 2.5 #4(b) "turns from east towards north": add "the wind now blows from the north".
