## HIGH

H1. C8S-5.2 [3] fill_blank. "Fig. 5.2 gives these article ranges: equality 14-18, exploitation 23 and 24, constitutional remedies 32. Counting every numbered article ... gives ___ articles." Key 8.
Problem: the stem hands over every number; the answer is 5+2+1 and needs no chapter fact. Maths trivia, not Social Science.
Fix: replace with a fill that needs a chapter fact (for example the article-free "A citizen whose fundamental right is denied may approach the ___" is too lookup; better a scenario whose answer is a category name that the student must infer, with ranges not given).

H2. C8S-5.3 [5] fill_blank. "A library has 60 extra books. Giving each of its 12 classes the same number, as the chapter's equality panel describes, means ___ books to each class." Key 5.
Problem: 60/12; the chapter fact (equality = same for all) is written into the stem. Answerable from the stem alone.
Fix: ask for the concept the split illustrates ("this equal split is an example of ___" would be a gimme too); better: give need data and ask which word ("equity") a need-based split is, or drop and use a different fill type.

H3. C8S-5.7 [5] fill_blank. "The first Fundamental Duties were added in 1976 and the 11th duty in 2002. The 11th duty was added ___ years after the first ten." Key 26.
Problem: both years are in the stem; pure subtraction. It also duplicates 5.7 [19](b), which asks the same 1976-1950 / 2002-1976 sums.
Fix: give neither year and ask for the year or the count the chapter fact supplies ("the 11th duty, about children's education, was added in ___"), or replace; then remove the repeat from [19](b).

H4. C8S-5.2 [15] short_answer 3 marks. "Using the table on page 154 and Fig. 5.2, give the article or articles and the broad category ... (i) forced labour; (ii) untouchability; (iii) right to approach courts."
Problem: scope_out says "Reciting from memory which article number carries which guarantee ... questions here ask recognition against the chapter's own table and Fig. 5.2, never recall of a bare number", and p.154 says "You need not memorise these". This item gives no table in the stem and asks the student to produce three article numbers. Three marks of pure recall of what the book says not to memorise.
Fix: give the guarantee and the category options and ask which category covers each case (recognition), or put the table lines in the stem and ask the student to decide which category and why.

H5. C8S-5.6 [12] short_answer 2 marks. "(a) Which articles does Fig. 5.2 give for freedom of religion and which for cultural and educational rights? (b) What three actions does the table on page 154 say Articles 25-28 cover?"
Problem: same scope_out violation (bare article-number recall plus a table not shown in the stem); (b) is a one-line lookup.
Fix: replace with a scenario asking which protection a situation breaches, or give the table line and ask for application.

H6. C8S-5.4 [9] short_answer 2 marks. "(b) A group of students keep shouting at a school meeting so that no one else can speak. Which part of that reason do they breach?" Key: "the part about harm to others".
Problem: the book's reason has three parts ("not misused or harmful to others or the nation"). Shouting down a meeting is equally "misuse" of the freedom. The pack itself says so: 5.4 [17] sample answer uses the same shouting example and writes "so that freedom is not misused". A student answering "misused" is defensible and would lose the mark.
Fix: change the scenario so only one part fits (a rumour that damages a named classmate is "harmful to others"), or accept either "misused" or "harmful to others" in the rubric and make (b) ask "name the part and justify it".

H7. C8S-5.6 [18] case study, part (c). Lines 1-4 given; "(c) Which two lines belong to the same right, and which right is it?" Key: Lines 1 and 2, freedom of religion.
Problem: Line 3 ("A government-aided school may promote the religion of its managers") is also about the right to freedom of religion (the school rule sits inside that right on pp.152-153). So lines 1+3 and 2+3 are equally defensible pairs. Also (a) and (b) climb nothing (obvious false line, then recite the rule), so the case has no real decision.
Fix: "Which two of the three correct lines..." is not possible as asked; instead ask "Lines 1, 2 and 3 all concern one right; name it and the article range" and add a genuine judgement part.

H8. C8S-5.7 [14] claim_check 3 marks. "Dhruv says: 'Dharma is simply the old word for duty, and in ancient texts everyone was guaranteed the same rights.'" Key: "Partly right".
Problem: both halves contradict the book. The book says dharma is "often translated as 'duty', although it has a much wider range of meaning", which contradicts "simply the old word"; and it says rights "were not guaranteed universally" (the single exception, the right to justice "in theory at least", does not make "the same rights" true). The key grants the first half as "partly accurate" to reach "partly right"; "No" is at least as defensible, so the verdict is not unique.
Fix: make the first half unambiguously true ("Dharma is often translated as 'duty'") and keep the second half false, which gives a clean "partly right"; or make both false and key "No".
