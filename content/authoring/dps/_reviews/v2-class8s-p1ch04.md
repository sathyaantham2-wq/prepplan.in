## HIGH

**H1. C8S-4.1 [7] (case study, exhibition label).**
Quote: label says "brave European explorers travelled the globe in a spirit of discovery, bringing progress to primitive lands and peacefully opening them to trade". (a) "Which ONE of the chapter's four motives does the label mention?" Key: scientific inquiry. (b) "Name two motives the label leaves out." Key: any two of political, economic, conversion.
Problem: the label also mentions trade ("opening them to trade"), and the chapter's economic-advantage motive is exactly new trade routes and markets. So the label names two motives. (a)'s premise ("one") is false, and (b)'s key lists economic advantage as an omitted motive when the label includes it. A careful student can answer "economic advantage" for (a) and be right, or object to (b).
Fix: delete "and peacefully opening them to trade" from the label (keep "peacefully" somewhere else, e.g. "bringing progress to primitive lands, with no more than a few peaceful voyages", or keep it for (c) as a separate clause such as "...bringing progress to primitive lands, and doing so peacefully."). Then (b)'s two omitted motives are political competition, economic advantage and conversion (any two), which is correct.

**H2. C8S-4.6 [15] (case study, three sources), part (b).**
Quote: "(b) The U.S. historian Will Durant called this wealth 'stolen wealth from India'. Which source's claim does his phrase sum up best?" Key: Source P (Adams).
Problem: second defensible answer. Source Q (Digby: "the plunder is proceeding far more outrageously...") and Source R (45 trillion dollars extracted) are also claims that wealth was taken. The key rests only on where the book places Durant's phrase (a gloss on Adams, p.97), which the stem does not give, so the item cannot tell a student who read Q or R as "stolen wealth" that she is wrong.
Fix: put the premise in the stem ("Durant used 'stolen wealth from India' to say that Britain's industrial revolution was funded at least partly by that wealth. Which source's claim does this sum up?"). Then P is the only one that mentions the industrial revolution, and the key is unarguable.
Related: parts (a), (c), (d) of this same item are answerable from the stem alone (see M-cases below).
