## MEDIUM

1. **12.2 [7]** "Which should she start with? ... Orion or the Pole Star" (January, Nagpur). Key reason 2 says the Pole Star "is harder for a beginner". The book (p.237) says "You may easily identify the Big Dipper and the Pole Star", so that reason contradicts the page and opens a second defensible reading. Fix: base both reasons on the book's season facts (Orion is best viewed Dec to April after sunset; the Big Dipper search is set for summer around 9 p.m.; the Pole Star is dim), and drop "harder for a beginner".
2. **12.2 [14]** claim check "The only reason people in big cities see so few stars is light pollution." Key is "Partly right"; a student who answers "Wrong, because of 'only'" is equally defensible and the rubric awards the verdict mark for "partly right". Fix: reword to "The main reason ... is light pollution" or give the verdict mark for either "wrong" or "partly right" with the two other causes.
3. **12.4 [17]** claim check "the Moon goes around the Earth more than 13 times" in 365 days. 365/27 = 13.5 gives 13 complete revolutions plus a half; "more than 13 times" can be read as 14 completed, so "Yes" is not unique. Fix: "at least 13 full times" or "about 13 and a half times", or ask "how many full revolutions".
4. **12.3 [5]** Venus case study: the family can choose 5:30 a.m. or 7:00 p.m., and the key assumes Venus is up at both times. The chapter (Activity 12.4) says Venus is found at dawn or at dusk with an app, never both on one day. Fix: state in the stem that an app shows Venus is above the horizon at both times (or at the western horizon only), otherwise the decision is not determinable. Also the last part (c) is an explanation, not the decision (the decision is part (a)); acceptable, but a final judgement part would fit the standard better.
