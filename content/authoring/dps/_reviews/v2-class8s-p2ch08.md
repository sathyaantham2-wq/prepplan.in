1. C8S-8.4 [19] (short_answer, 2 marks): "Mughal miniature paintings are also known as court paintings. (a) What name does the chapter give them? (b) Using (a), state what made them possible." The stem hands over the answer to (a), and (b) is a one-line lookup ("Mughal royal patronage"). It is stem-answerable recall.
   Fix: replace. For example give a painting with a court scene and ask what it shows about who paid for such work, and what separates it from Rajput work. Or drop it.

2. C8S-8.4 [14] (long_answer, 5): "three heads: typical themes, the influence that shaped each, and who supported the work." The book names a supporter only for Mughal painting (royal patronage, Akbar's atelier). For Rajput painting it names none. The key invents "Rajput painting was shaped by its patrons' taste and demand", but the book says that only about the exchange of elements. Step 3 "Patronage of each genre" therefore cannot be earned from the book.
   Fix: change the third head to "what the book says about the two genres' contact with each other" (exchange of style, technique, colour, elements; Bikaner), or ask patronage for the Mughal genre only. Keep the step marks at 5.

3. C8S-8.7 [14] (claim_check, "Partly right"): Faisal says "The Banarasi GI tag lets any weaver call his sari Banarasi." The claim is one sentence and wholly false: the chapter says the tag restricts who may use the name. The key manufactures a "right half" (the tag gives weavers rights over their knowledge), which Faisal never claimed. The correct verdict is No, so the key is wrong.
   Fix: either change the verdict to No, or make the claim genuinely two-part, e.g. "The GI tag gives Banaras weavers rights over the knowledge they hold, and lets any weaver anywhere call a silk sari Banarasi." That is true then false, which supports "Partly right".

4. C8S-8.7 [12] (short_answer, 2): "Give two ways in which schools of this period were unlike schools today". The book states one: schools were "quite unlike what we have today", most likely a priest's house or temple mandapa. The second key point, "students lived with their teachers", comes from the sentence about gurukulas, mathas and similar institutions. The book never presents it as a contrast with today.
   Fix: ask "State the setting the chapter gives for the schools Della Valle describes, and two institutions where students lived with their teachers." Or ask for one way.
