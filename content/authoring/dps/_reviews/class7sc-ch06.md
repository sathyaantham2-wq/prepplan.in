## HIGH (wrong key / second correct answer / fact error)

1. C7SC-6.2 case study #19 (Priya and Zoya, 4 March). Part (a) reads "give the date of Priya's next period (1 April)". The answer is printed in the question, so the mark is free and the part tests nothing. Fix: delete "(1 April)". (The arithmetic is correct: 4 Mar + 28 d = 1 Apr; 5 days from 4 Mar ends 8 Mar; 4 Mar + 34 d = 7 Apr.)

No wrong keys found otherwise. Case-study maths in 6.4 #19 checks: 60x12x25 = 18,000; 60x9x25 = 13,500; leftover 4,500 / 300 = 15 girls.

## MEDIUM

2. C7SC-6.2 match #9 (Secondary sexual characteristics / Puberty / Menstruation / Menstrual cycle). Distractor "1-b, 2-c, 3-d, 4-a" (menstruation = recurs every 28-30 days; cycle = discharge 3-7 days) is defensible from the book's own Nutshell ("discharge of blood generally after every 28-30 days, a process called menstruation") and Exercise 3(i). Only the main text separates cycle from phase. Fix: reword Column II c/d so the cycle is "whole process that repeats" and menstruation is "the days of blood discharge, 3-7 days", with no "recurs every 28-30 days" on either side, or drop the cycle/menstruation pairing from the match and keep it in the short answer. The 2-mark "differentiate cycle and menstruation" (#12) has the same exposure; accept the Nutshell reading in the key note.
3. C7SC-6.1 case study #20 (Kavya's sapling). Grandmother says "It must grow first", which hands over part (a). Also a 3-month mango sapling flowering is unrealistic as a puzzle (mango takes years). Fix: remove the grandmother's line; make it a 2-year-old tulsi/marigold seedling, or ask the student to explain. The "cousin" has no gender, so part (c) has two possible answers (girls: only slight voice change). Fix: say "her 15-year-old male cousin".
4. C7SC-6.1 case study #19 (Pune slips). The slip counts (18, 9, 8, 5) are never used, parts (a)-(c) are one-fact recall, nothing climbs. Fix: use the data, e.g. "which change did most students notice, what fraction mentioned voice or skin", and make (c) or (d) a reasoning step.
5. Cases whose early parts are answered by the stem or are plain recall: C7SC-6.3 #19 (Tanvir: part (c) "two ways" is shown by Meera and Joseph in the stem; part (a) is read straight off the stem; part (b) "increased sensitivity" competes with "strong emotions"); 6.3 #18 (Kabir): (a) and (b) are read off the stem; 6.4 #20 (Mohini): Plan B strictly dominates Plan A, so the decision is not a decision; 6.5 #19 (Ishaan): plans (i) banned and (ii) free are straw men. Fix: give the plans a real trade-off (cost, time, one benefit lost) and make the justification use two earlier answers.
6. Templated bank. Every concept has the identical skeleton in the identical order: 4 plain MCQ, 2 scenario, 1 statements, 1 AR, 5 short answers, then claim-check "X says '...'. Do you agree?", a "Describe/Write a three-line ..." reverse item, a "Justify the statement" 5-marker, and two case studies. Specific repeats:
   - Statements: in 5 of 6 items Statement 3 is the false one and the key is always "1 and 2 only" (6.2-6.6). Fix: vary which statement is false and have some items with only one true (keys "1 only", "2 and 3 only", "3 only").
   - Plain MCQ "Which ... is NOT ..." in 6.2, 6.3 and 6.5 (all tagged reverse, see 11).
   - 6.5 repeats the same fact four times: MCQ #3 (best response to online bullying), short answer #13 (two steps if bullied), short answer #16 (three things Rani should do) and case study #19(c). Replace #16 with a different move (e.g. compare two responses to a bullying message).
   - Four-of-six scenario and claim items end with "talk to parent or teacher".
7. Longest-option cue. In about 14 MCQs the correct option is clearly the longest (e.g. 6.2 #1 secondary sexual characteristics 81 vs 52/46/37 characters, 6.5 #2 cyberbullying 124 vs 36-51, 6.6 #1 hormones, 6.4 #6 cricket coach 76 vs 37-53, 6.3 #6 Kiran). Fix: lengthen the distractors to similar length or shorten the key.
8. C7SC-6.5 long answer #18 ("physical activity and a balanced social life are as important as nutrition"). The book calls nutrition "of utmost importance"; it never ranks exercise or social life as equal. Fix: "...are also important along with nutrition."
9. C7SC-6.4 long answer #19 ("Using Table 6.3"). Table 6.3 in the book is a fill-in activity with blank cells, so the mapping the key gives (millets under calcium, proteins "growth, strength and energy") is partly author inference, and a student without the book cannot know the table. Fix: state the table content in the stem, or ask about "the nutrients and foods in the chapter". The key also adds "carbohydrates give energy", which this chapter does not teach (Class 6).
10. C7SC-6.2 short answer #14 ("What is a myth? Give one myth ... and say why it is wrong"). Three tasks, two marks, and the step marks omit the definition of "myth". Fix: drop "What is a myth?" or add the step. C7SC-6.2 long answer #18: steps include "Internal change: maturation of reproductive parts" which the question never asks for; add it to the stem or replace the step.

17. 6.3 short answer #12 step marks "Better choices" and "Thoughtful responses" split one idea into two marks.

19. 6.6 long answer #18 step 5 "Links physical and emotional changes" is vague; name the link (hormones also influence mood).
20. 6.5 short answer #9 (a forwarded classmate photo is cyberbullying?) is arguable: the book's definition is harassment by sharing personal information without consent; add "to mock her" to the stem so the answer is unambiguous.
