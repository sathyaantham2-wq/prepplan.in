## MEDIUM
- M1 (4.6 #12, assertion_reason) Two defensible answers. A: "1 / 7 never ends." R: "1 / 7 = 0.142857142857..., whose repeating block has length 6." Keyed "both true, R is not the correct explanation". A repeating block of digits is a perfectly good reason that the division never ends (a non-zero repeat means no remainder 0), so a careful student can choose "R is the correct explanation". The textbook explanation is the repeating remainder, which R does not mention. Fix: make R something true but off the point, e.g. "the digit sum of the block 142857 is 27", or make R the correct explanation ("the remainder 1 returns after 6 steps and the division never reaches remainder 0") and re-key to "explains".
- M2 (case studies whose decision is forced, 4.4 #12, 4.5 #12, 4.6 #16, 4.7 #20) The calculation parts settle the choice, so (d) is not a real decision. 4.4 #12 (d) hands the answer ("leaving out two classes is unfair"); 4.5 #12 (d): the budget has "no money left for tolls", so the hill road is the only option and the 6-minute spare is a footnote; 4.6 #16: only 1/37 has a 3-digit block, parts (a)-(c) about 3/7 play no part in the decision; 4.7 #20: the error figures (0.12 vs 0.88 days) decide Rule B, and the "simpler" consideration is named but never costed.
