## MEDIUM
1. 4.4 #13(d) second defensible selection. Case needs "the 20 objects" for a case titled "What was new in the age of the mahajanapadas". Part (a) itself counts the 6 copper coins among the 26 finds tied to the Second Urbanisation, but the key then drops them as "later issues" and requires exactly 12 iron + 8 silver. A student who chooses 12 iron + 6 copper coins + 2 silver coins is consistent with the stem and the book ("later, coins of copper, gold ... were also made"). Fix: state in the stem that the case is for "the earliest innovations" or give the case 18 places so only 12 iron + 6 silver fit.
2. 4.6 #21(d) the aim forces only Mohan's project. The exhibition aims to show connections beyond India; only Mohan's (south, overseas trade, Rs 30,000) does that, and the fund is Rs 75,000. The key funds Karan + Mohan, and the justification for adding Karan (the road to the south) is not an aim stated in the stem. A student funding Mohan alone, or Mohan plus the cheapest extra, loses the mark. Fix: add a second aim ("and show how the south was linked by road to the Ganga plains") so the pair is forced, or accept any affordable set that includes Mohan with a stated reason.
3. 4.4 #22 key withholds a mark on a claim the book does not make. Step 4 and the coin line say acceptance "three regions away" is less certain because the chapter "mentions neighbours' coins". The book says coins "were exchanged across different regions and even with other parts of the world", so the coin reaching a distant city is supported, and the key contradicts itself ("supported ... but acceptance there is not shown"). Also "a Buddhist nun around 600 BCE" is a doubtful date for the Buddhist school the book dates only loosely. Fix: say "around 500 BCE" or "a monk or nun of a school of thought", and make the limit "the chapter does not describe this journey or its cost".
4. 4.4 #2 stem and key pull in two directions. The headman is placed in the late 2nd millennium BCE, but the book says iron tools were already widespread by then, so "expect the change to be gradual, not immediate" (the expected conclusion in (b)) does not follow for him; the book's few centuries have already passed. Fix: place him in the early 2nd millennium BCE, "just after the techniques were perfected".
5. 4.3 #12 (Veerapur, 5 marks) is a hybrid the book does not classify. A hereditary ruler plus a council that votes on war: the book says a gana's assembly "took important decisions through discussion, and, if necessary, through vote" and also selected the rajah. Feature (3) therefore points to a gana while (2) and (4) point to a monarchy, and "monarchy" as the single decision is the key's preference, not the book's. A well-argued "mixed, leans monarchy" or "cannot be placed" is defensible. Fix: drop the vote on war, or ask "which features are monarchy markers and which gana markers" without forcing the label.
6. 4.6 #19(d) (Ravi, port vs workshop) the key picks the port (Rs 360 more, 7.5 percent) while its own note says "the gain is small" and mentions effort and risk; a student choosing the workshop with those reasons is equally consistent with the numbers and the book. Step line requires "chooses the port". Fix: make the port's gain unambiguous (e.g. Rs 5,000 more) or accept either choice with a reason that uses (a) and (c).
7. 4.2 #6 (Group P / Group Q) asks what the groups "show about where the strongest states stood in relation to the edges of the list".
