## C7S-6.6 The judiciary (20 read)

Keys: all recomputed against pp.160-161; all keyed answers are the ones the page supports. The weaknesses are factual realism in the case studies and one arguable A-R.
- MEDIUM, Q19 part (b): key says the family can go to court because "their rights were violated", citing the chapter's "if fundamental rights are violated, we can go to court". A plot of land and a government office is a property dispute, and the right to property is not a fundamental right in India. The key therefore rests on a wrong legal premise. Fix: make the ground "the legal process for acquiring the land was not followed, and the rule of law applies to the government too" (the book's own tribal-land example, p.150, uses "due legal process"), and keep part (a) as the rule of law.
- MEDIUM, Q18 stem: a "district court" hears a challenge to a new state law on constitutional grounds (third matter). Judicial review of a state law is done by the High Court or Supreme Court, not a district court; the book does not say which court, so use "a High Court" (or just "a court") to avoid teaching a wrong picture. Also matter two ("her employer's rule denies promotion to married women") would be judicial review if the employer is a government department (Muthamma's own case); say "a private company's rule" so that only "protecting rights" fits.
- LOW, Q7 (A-R): R ("referee when the government or others break the law") plausibly explains why independence is needed, but the book never makes that link and a student can defend "true, not the explanation". Two defensible options. Fix: make R a clearly explaining sentence taken from the page ("It is not controlled by the government or politicians, which helps keep justice fair and equal") or make A-R deliberately "true but does not explain" (A: courts can interpret laws; R: the judiciary is independent).
- LOW, Q3: "Peacefully" against "By force, through the police / vote in legislature / orders of executive" is a one-word recall with no real chapter confusion. Replace with the difference between judicial review and the executive's implementation of laws.
- LOW, Q9(b) and Q12 repeat the same three benefits (check on unfair actions, rights, peaceful resolution). Make Q12 an application (name the benefit shown by a described court case).
- LOW, Q10: the "referee" key adds reasoning the book does not give; credit as application, state this in the step.
Verdict: sound keys; two MEDIUM accuracy fixes in the case studies.
