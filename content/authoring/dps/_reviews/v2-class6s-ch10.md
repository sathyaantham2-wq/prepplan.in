## C6S-10.1 Governance, government and law (21 items)

HIGH
- 10.1 [18] (ordering, 5m) "Arrange these five events ... S, R, T, Q, P". Key is not unique. The chapter gives no
  sequence between "organise life with the rule", "ensure it is followed" and "citizens ask for a change". S,R,T,P,Q
  is just as defensible (drivers ask for a change after the rule is announced and before checks begin, or while
  checks are on). Fix: ask only for what the book fixes (S first, R before T/Q), or change P to a clearly later event
  ("a year later, after the rule has been in force...") and make Q unambiguously before it; or turn into a
  classify-each-event item (which part of governance is each).

MEDIUM
- 10.1 [10] (compare, 5m) points (ii) "law applies to everyone in the society" and (iii) "parents see that the home
  rule is followed" and (v) "ignoring laws affects the whole society, not one family" are not stated in this book.
  The book only says laws are some of the more important rules a government makes. Fix: keep (i),(iv),(v-as-book) and
  replace (ii)/(iii) with points the book supports (example of what government does: arrests, court, fine, jail;
  citizens have a say), or accept any sensible answer and drop "who has to follow it".
- 10.1 [15] (case study) parts (a) and (b) are answerable from the newsletter extract alone ("which line shows
  teachers must..."; "which line is an exam rule") and part (c) needs only reading comprehension. Nothing needs the
  chapter's ideas except a closing echo. Fix: make (a)/(b) ask which governance part or which chapter idea each line
  shows (e.g. staff handbook update = rules bind both sides; Sports Day change = citizens' say), and make (c) use
  "not set once and for all".
- 10.1 [0] (case study) part (c) "what is the committee acting as" is a one-line definition and the stem gives away
  (a)-(b). Part (d) is a strawman decision: the survey says all 120 flats have read the rule, so the notices
  option is dead on arrival, and Rs 500 left is trivial. Fix: make both spends defensible (e.g. survey shows 70 of 120
  have read it, 14 complaints, Rs 9,000 buys only one), and ask the student to justify using (b).
- 10.1 repetition: "a small group acting as a government / the three parts of governance" is tested in [0], [1],
  [11](b), [13] and [18]; "rules for students and for teachers" in [11](a), [14](b), [15](a), [17]; "employers also
  bound" in [2], [9], [14](a), [17]. More than two per idea. Fix: drop or rewrite [14] (it is two recall lines) and
  [11]; keep [13] as the single "act as a government" item.
- 10.1 easy in disguise (answer is one line the book states, Bloom label too high): [7] (sentence completion of the
  book's own comparison, three absurd distractors), [8] (the book's own "society would not be able to function" with
  Only-the-roads style distractors), [3] (A "fixed once and for all" is a strawman; R is a verbatim line), [14],
  [12](a). Fix: [7] and [8] to a real situation needing a choice between two close ideas (e.g. rule-maker vs
  rule-enforcer); [3] to an A/R where R is true but does not explain A.

LOW
- 10.1 [1] distractors "the committee is the governance" and "is a law" are absurd and not real chapter
  confusions except the governance/government swap; replace with swaps within the chapter's three terms.
- 10.1 [19] "NOT from the chapter" relies on what Fig. 10.1/10.2 pictures show (cinema hall not visible in the
  extracted text). Safe on the text but unverifiable on the figures; prefer a distractor the text certainly lacks.
- 10.1 [12](b) "even without anyone meaning harm" goes slightly beyond the book (it says disagreements and disorder
  arise when many people live together). Fix: drop the phrase.
- 10.1 [2] statements 1 and 3 are verbatim recall; only statement 2 needs thought.
