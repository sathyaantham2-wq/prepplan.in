## HIGH

**H1. C8SC-8.1 index 19 (bars X, Y, Z).** Key needs "Y is brass, second metal zinc". The stem says only "Y and Z both contain copper; X contains no copper...; Z contains tin" and "the owner knows only this". Nothing says Y contains no tin, so Y could be bronze too (both Y and Z copper and tin). Part (a) therefore has no single answer, and part (c) (tell Y from Z) depends on it. The author's claim that every classification is given in the stem fails here. Smallest fix: add "Y contains no tin and no iron" (or "the three bars are three different alloys, and Y has no tin") to the stem.

**H2. C8SC-8.2 index 5 (yellow powder sold as 'sulfur', some grains dissolve, the rest settle unchanged).** The key "it is not pure, as its grains do not all behave alike" is not warranted. Any pure substance added to too little liquid shows exactly this (a saturated solution: some dissolves, the rest settles unchanged). So "pure" is also defensible, and the wrong option "It is pure, because dissolving is only a change of state" is not clearly wrong either. Smallest fix: state that plenty of liquid was used, more liquid was added, and the same grains still did not dissolve, while others did (grains of two kinds that differ in a property), or replace the item.

**H3. C8SC-8.2 index 18 part (c) ("Which of the three is made of more than one element").** Items compared: oxygen, common salt, salt dissolved in water. Salt water also contains more than one element (sodium, chlorine, hydrogen, oxygen), so the key "common salt only" is not the only defensible answer. The intended idea is "which one is a compound". Smallest fix: ask "which of the three is a compound, and why can its elements not be separated by evaporation or filtering?" and adjust the step line.

**H4. C8SC-8.3 index 20 part (b) ("explain why water cannot be a building block in the same sense").** The book's p.130 says "Elements and compounds are the building blocks of matter", so water (a compound) is a building block per this book. The key (water is not a building block because it breaks down) contradicts the page, and a student quoting p.130 has a second defensible answer. The item also repeats 8.3 index 6(b) (why water is not an element). Smallest fix: replace (b) with a new idea (for example, what the phrase says about hydrogen and oxygen, or why a compound can still be broken into building blocks), or drop the item and write a different one.
