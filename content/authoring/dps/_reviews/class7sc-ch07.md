## HIGH (wrong key / second defensible answer / fact error)

1. C7SC-7.5 case study #20 (Kullu bukhari), part (b): "Name the process by which the children two metres away feel warm, and say why it cannot be conduction." The stem describes a stove burning in a closed winter room with a pipe; the room air is warmed by convection too, and the book itself (Fascinating Facts, p. 97) says a bukhari involves all three processes. Only radiation is keyed, so a student answering "convection" is defensible. Fix: add the discipline used in MCQ 7.5 #5: "...feel warmth on their faces though no hot air is blowing towards them", or ask "which process brings the warmth they feel directly on their skin from the stove".

No wrong key, arithmetic error or fact error found.

## MEDIUM

2. C7SC-7.5 AR #8 (A: dark clothes more comfortable in winter; R: the Sun's heat reaches the Earth through radiation), keyed "true but R not the explanation". R is a link in the explanation (dark surfaces absorb the radiated heat), so "R explains A" is arguable. Fix: choose an R that is clearly unrelated, e.g. "Woollen fabric traps air in its pores", or make R "Dark surfaces absorb more of the heat that falls on them" and key it as the explanation.
3. C7SC-7.2 AR #8 (A: two thin blankets keep us warmer than one thick; R: woollen fabric traps air in its pores), keyed "not the explanation". The book's reason for A is trapped air between the blankets, and R is also trapped air, so a student can fairly say R explains A. Also A is stated as a universal fact (the book says "we prefer"). Fix: R = "Wool is made from the hair of sheep" or R = "Air trapped between the two blankets is a poor conductor" keyed as the explanation, and give A "...because of ..." free wording like "Two thin blankets are preferred to one thick blanket for warmth".
