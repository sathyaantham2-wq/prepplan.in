## MEDIUM
- C7S-6.6 Q18 (case study): premise is internally incoherent. Matter 1 (two farmers) and matter 2 (a private company's rule) have no government party, yet (b)/(c) rest on "the government accepts and follows the court's orders" as evidence of the rule of law. Matter 2 also puts a fundamental-right claim against a private company, which the chapter never teaches (it speaks of rights against the government). Fix: make matter 2 a government department's rule (as in the Muthamma story) and word (c) around the matter(s) where the government is a party.
- C7S-6.4 Q2 (MCQ): the key text says "An IAS officer and an anganwadi worker" but no option is that pair; the listed correct option is "An IAS and an IPS officer". Both are all-permanent pairs so no wrong answer is awarded, but the rationale does not match the options. Fix: rewrite the key text to "An IAS and an IPS officer ...".
- C7S-6.4 Q18(d): key says the ministers (not the officers) must answer if the promise is broken, "because their power depends on the people's trust". The book defines accountability for "those in power" and gives officers duties to implement and address grievances, so "both, with the ministers answerable politically" is also defensible. Fix: ask "who decided the 6-day promise" or accept both with reasoning.
