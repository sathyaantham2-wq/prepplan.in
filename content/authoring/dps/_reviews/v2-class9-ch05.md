- 5.1: regular hexagon turns 60 degrees and 6 lines is tested four times (Q8, Q11, Q17, Q19), with Q17 and Q19 nearly the same case study.
- 5.2: "right angle gives circumcentre at the hypotenuse midpoint" appears six times (Q1 with 7-24-25, Q6 with 9-12, Q7 part 1, Q10, Q15, Q17, Q18).
- 5.3: the isosceles triangle with base 24 and height 18 (radius 13) is Q3 (MCQ) and again all of Q19; "perpendicular bisects the chord" is Q9, Q13, Q14, Q15 (Q15 restates the 5.1 Q16 closing step).
- 5.4: Q18 reuses the Q1 data exactly (radius 25 with chords 14, 40, 48, distances 24, 15, 7).
- 5.5: angle in a semicircle is tested in Q2, Q7, Q10, Q12, Q15, Q16, Q18, Q19.
Suggest swapping one or two items per concept (for example 5.3 Q19 with new numbers, 5.4 Q18 with a different radius, one of the 5.2 right-triangle items for an obtuse or acute circumcentre case).

M2. C9M-5.1 Q19 (hexagon logo vs plain circle) is a strawman case study. Part (d) answer is fixed by the stem: nobody picks a design where the spin cannot be seen. Parts (a) to (c) are only restatements of earlier items. C9M-5.1 Q17(c) has the same flaw (the "looks the same at all times" story copies the book's remark rather than testing it). Replace with a real trade-off or fold into a 3-mark item.

M3. C9M-5.5 Q11 depends on the book's figure. It says "E outside arc AFB" and "C is the centre" but never says what arc AFB is, which side A and B lie on relative to line DC, or that the angles subtract (answer needs A and B on the same side of DC). State it fully in words (for example "A and B lie on the same side of the line DC, which meets the circle again at E") per the rule "state what the item needs".

## LOW

L1. C9M-5.1 Q5 (assertion_reason, key "both true, R not the explanation"): defensible but not airtight. Two reflections in diameters compose to a rotation, so a strong student can argue R does explain A. The book states them separately, so the key holds, but consider a cleaner R.

L2. C9M-5.1 Q6 (fill_blank 18.6) is easy in disguise: it is the book's own "longest chord is the diameter" line. Same for Q2 (18 m vs 17 m diameter). Acceptable at Hard only because the setting hides the diameter; raise the demand if possible (for example give the radius as a decimal expression or a perimeter-free data item).

L3. C9M-5.3 Q18 (Rs 60 for 5 threads plus Rs 15 each, vs Rs 80 for 6) is contrived pricing, and the sample answer's "Kit B saves buying twice" has no basis in the data. Drop that clause.

L4. C9M-5.4 Q16 (shortest chord through an interior point is perpendicular to OA) goes a step beyond what the book states; the justification given is sound and self-contained, so it stays, but it is an extension, not a book theorem.

L5. C9M-5.5 Q20(d) answer cites "Theorem 10" by number. The numbering is this book's, so it is true to the source, but the question stem itself should not require it. Part (b) (angle OBC) is not used by (c) or (d), so it is padding.

L6. C9M-5.6 Q19 answer says "a square layout" for Plot 2, but 90, 90, 90, 90 only fixes a rectangle. Say "rectangular layout". Also Plot 3 is an obvious reject; fine as the cost trap, just note the decision is mostly forced.

L7. C9M-5.6 Q20 asks (d) for a "one-line rule" but the sample answer has two lines of caveats ("48 degrees or less"). The rubric still works; tighten wording. Also the stem's "rocks lie inside the circle" assumes the circle is for this side only, which is stated ("on this side"); acceptable.
