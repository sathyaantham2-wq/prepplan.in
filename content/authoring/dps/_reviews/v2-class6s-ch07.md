## HIGH (2)

1. C6S-7.2 [6] fill_blank: "names only five of them, so it names fewer than one in every ___ janas of that list." Key: 6.
   Problem: 5 of "over 30" is below one in 6, but it is also below one in 5, one in 4, one in 3 and one in 2, so every integer from 2 to 6 is a true answer. A machine match on "6" will mark correct answers wrong.
   Fix: ask for a determinate value, e.g. "The chapter names 5 of the 30 janas, which is 1 in every ___ (at the minimum count of 30)." or "...the chapter names about 1 in every ___ of at least 30 janas listed, so the tightest whole-number statement is 1 in ___". Simplest: "Five of the 30 janas make up the fraction 1/___ of the list."

2. C6S-7.2 [12] case study (model village, Rs 1,500), part (d): key says "It must add a sabha scene (Rs 350)... 350 + 2 x 90 = Rs 530 ... Rs 200 left". The stem gives a price only for the samiti scene (Rs 350); no price for a sabha scene is stated, so the answer rests on an invented number. A student can also argue the samiti (assembly) scene already shows a gathering.
   Fix: add "a sabha (gathering) scene Rs 350" to the price list in the stem, and make the samiti and sabha cards distinct, or change (d) to "which clue-word is still missing" and compute with the stated price.
