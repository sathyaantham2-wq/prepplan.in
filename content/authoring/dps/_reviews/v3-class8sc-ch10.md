## HIGH (2, both introduced by the fix pass)

1. C8SC-10.1 [2] multi_statement, "Statement 2: A concave mirror and a convex mirror lying face up on a table look different when seen from directly above." The key makes S2 false (answer "3 only"). But looking down at a face-up concave and a face-up convex mirror, the images in them differ (enlarged erect or inverted against small erect), so a student can fairly call S2 true and choose "2 and 3 only". The intended idea is the circular outline, but the statement says "look different". Smallest fix: "...have different outlines when seen from directly above" (false: both are circular, consistent with [7] row 4), keep the key "3 only".

2. C8SC-10.6 [18] assertion_reason, "A: We can read a book held close and also see a far-away hill clearly. R: Our eye has a convex lens inside it." Key: both true, R is NOT the explanation. The book says (p.165) the eye's convex lens "can change its shape, which is what allows us to read a book or see something far away". R is the first half of that sentence, so "R explains A" is also defensible, and the "not the explanation" verdict depends on a detail the stem leaves out. Smallest fix: either make R "Our eye has a lens that can change its shape" (then key: R explains, and the AR key spread becomes 2 explains, 2 not, 1, 1; change another item if a balance is wanted), or keep "not explanation" by changing R to a clearly unrelated true line (for example "Eyeglasses are lenses"), which does not hand over the explanation.

Fix: replace (c) with a book-grounded prediction (the beam returns when the paper is flattened again, and what that shows), or ask "why must the beam be watched on paper kept in the plane of the incident beam".
