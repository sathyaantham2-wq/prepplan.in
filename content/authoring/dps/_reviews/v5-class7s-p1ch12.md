## HIGH

1. C7S-12.1 #18 (short_answer, 2 marks). Key (a) reads "Yes. The glossary defines trade as buying and selling or exchange ... so the swap is trade." Ritu's claim is that a swap is NOT trade, and the question asks whether she is right. The answer "Yes" says she is right, then the same sentence says the swap is trade. The key contradicts itself. Marks step 1 ("the swap is trade because trade includes exchange of goods") is correct, so only the opening word is wrong, but a marker or student reading "Yes" gets the verdict backwards. Fix: change "Yes." to "No, Ritu is wrong." (or "No. The glossary defines trade as ... so the swap is trade.").

No other wrong key and no second defensible answer found in any MCQ, multi_statement, assertion_reason, match or fill_blank item.

## MEDIUM

1. C7S-12.4 #17 (mcq, rev). Stem: "Which statement from the chapter corrects him?" The correct option, "Only retailers sell to the final consumers", is not a statement in the chapter, and the key admits it ("the word 'only' is the contrast, not a quotation"). A careful student could pick "Wholesalers supply goods to the shops near households" because it is the real chapter sentence about wholesalers. It does not correct him, so the key holds, but the stem promises something the option does not deliver. Fix: change the stem to "Which statement, based on the chapter, corrects him?", or reword the option to the chapter's own sentence about retailers ("Retailers sell goods to final consumers like us, in smaller quantities").

2. C7S-12.7 #7 (mcq). Answer is a label-reading lookup: allergen declaration. The chapter's figure lists the label items in one place, so a student who has read that figure answers in a few seconds. The allergy story adds some application, but the item is close to a one-line recall. Fix: make the distractors harder (for example a shopper who needs to know how long the flour stays good vs one who needs to know whether it is from one batch), or add a second step.

3. C7S-12.3 #18 (mcq, figure). The figure item relies on which West Asia entry is an export and which is an import. In the text extraction the numbered lists of Fig. 12.10 are interleaved, so the split (refined petroleum = India's export, crude and fertilizers = India's import) is inferred from numbering, not read directly. It agrees with real trade and with the 5 exports / 6 imports count, so it is probably right. Fix: have someone open the page image for Fig. 12.10 (p.256) and confirm before loading.
