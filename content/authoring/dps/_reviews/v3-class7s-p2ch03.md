## MEDIUM
1. 3.3 #17 (5m, "four kinds of evidence that kings were not constantly at war"): two of the four demanded kinds do not support the claim. Al-Masudi's remark (innumerable troops and elephants; respect for Muslims) is about military strength and religious tolerance, and Nrupatunga Road is a lasting memory of a king's name, not evidence of peace. The key forces a student to present them as proof. The stem also hands the four heads. Fix: ask for evidence the Rashtrakutas "created wonders and patronised learning" (temple, poetry, patronage of three faiths and three languages, the road name as legacy), or change the claim to "the Rashtrakutas left lasting marks".
2. 3.7 #18(b) (case study, guild ships): key says traders from West Asia would use the west-coast route and cites the copper plates. The book sentence places the copper-plate inscriptions in the "similar activity ... on the east coast under the Cheras", so a student quoting the book can defend the east route (or note the text conflicts with geography). The book probably has a slip (Cheras are on the Kerala coast), but the item cannot rest on a correction of the book. Fix: drop the copper plates from (b) and ask which route links to Iran, Iraq and east Africa (the book states this explicitly), or ask only "what do the inscriptions show about India" (a hub of trade).
3. Repetition (3.4 and 3.5): in 3.4 Kalhana's "free from love or hatred" ideal is the point of #4, #5, #8, #11 and #16, and Didda's rule is used in #3, #4, #11, #13, #14 and #18; in 3.5 the Ravikirti-inscription exaggeration is the point of #6, #14, #17(c), #19 and #18, and Xuanzang's 100 monasteries / 10,000 priests of Kanchi is used in #4, #13(b) and #20. Several 1-mark items test the same single sentence. Fix: keep one MCQ and one long item per idea, and replace the rest with other facts (3.4: Shankaracharya and the mathas, Lalitaditya; 3.5: Mahendravarman's play, Dandin, Mamallapuram trade, Eastern Chalukya clashes).
4. 3.4 #19 (5m): "Compare Shankaracharya's advaita vedanta with Kashmir Shaivism". The book gives Shankaracharya's teaching (brahman, maya, four mathas) but says nothing about Kashmir Shaivism's teaching, only that its scholars (Abhinavagupta) wrote on philosophy, poetry, arts and aesthetics. The key therefore compares how they spread, which the stem does not ask. A student who compares doctrines must go beyond the book, a student who stays in the book misses the question. Fix: "Compare how Shankaracharya's teaching and Kashmir Shaivism's scholars spread their influence, and what the two show about intellectual life".
