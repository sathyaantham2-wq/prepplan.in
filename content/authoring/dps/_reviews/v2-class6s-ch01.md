## MEDIUM

1. **C6S-1.4, 4.20 (claim_check, Neha).** Claim: "The idea of a prime meridian came from Europe in 1884; before that, nobody measured longitude from a zero line." Key says "Partly right", with the right part "Greenwich became the international standard in 1884". But Neha did not claim only that. She claimed the *idea* started in 1884 and that nobody used a zero line before. The book says India's madhya rekha came many centuries earlier. So "No" is equally defensible, and a student who writes "No, India had one" loses marks. Fix: make the claim genuinely mixed, e.g. "Greenwich became the international prime meridian in 1884, and it was the first prime meridian ever used" (first half true, second half false), or change the verdict to No.
2. **C6S-1.4, 4.4 (assertion_reason).** A: Ujjayini meridian became the reference in all Indian texts. R: Varahamihira lived and worked in Ujjayini 1,500 years ago. Key: both true, R does not explain A. The book places the two facts side by side and says Ujjayini was a reputed astronomy centre, so "R explains A" (the astronomer's work there is part of why the place was a reference) is arguable. Fix: use an R that is clearly true but unrelated, e.g. "Greenwich was chosen as the international prime meridian in 1884".
